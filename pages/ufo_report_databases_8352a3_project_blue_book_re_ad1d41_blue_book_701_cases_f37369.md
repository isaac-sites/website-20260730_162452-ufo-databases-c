## Introduction

The 701 unresolved cases in [Project Blue Book]({{ 'blue-book/' | relative_url }}) are often misunderstood as a list of the Air Force's "best UFO cases" or as official evidence of extraterrestrial craft. In reality, they represent a residual category within a much larger investigative database. Between 1947 and 1969, the Air Force recorded 12,618 UFO reports. Most were eventually attributed to conventional causes, while others lacked sufficient information for meaningful analysis. The remaining 701 cases were those that investigators believed contained enough information to examine but could not confidently match to a known explanation at the time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

As a dataset, the 701 "unknowns" are valuable because they preserve unresolved observations rather than because they demonstrate a single unexplained phenomenon. They include reports from military personnel, commercial and military pilots, radar operators, police officers and civilians, collected over more than two decades under changing investigative practices. The cases vary enormously in quality, detail and credibility, making the collection far more heterogeneous than the simple number "701" suggests.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/news/articles/project-blue-book-50th-anniversary" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesPublic Interest in UFOs Persists 50 Years After Project Blue Book Termination &#124; National ArchivesDecember 5, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 5, 2019</span></span></span>

## How the Final Total Was Calculated

The figure of 701 was the cumulative total of reports that remained officially classified as "[unidentified]({{ 'unidentified/' | relative_url }})" when Project Blue Book closed in December 1969. It was not a separate investigation or curated shortlist assembled afterwards. Each case entered the total because it survived the Air Force's evaluation process without receiving a satisfactory conventional identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

This number should also be understood alongside Blue Book's broader classification system:

* **Identified:** investigators concluded that the object was an aircraft, balloon, astronomical object, meteorological phenomenon or another conventional cause.
* **Insufficient data:** the report lacked enough information for a meaningful evaluation.
* **Unidentified:** investigators believed the available information was sufficient to attempt identification, yet no explanation adequately fit the reported characteristics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://prologue.blogs.archives.gov/2018/04/16/ufos-natural-explanations/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: prologue.blogs.archives.gov">[Pieces of History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">prologue.blogs.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pieces of History UFOs: Natural Explanations – Pieces of History</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pieces of HistoryUFOs: Natural Explanations – Pieces of HistoryApril 16, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2018</span></span></span>

The distinction between "insufficient data" and "unidentified" is important. The 701 cases were not simply reports with missing information. At least according to Blue Book's own procedures, they were cases considered detailed enough to investigate but not detailed enough to reach a convincing conventional conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://prologue.blogs.archives.gov/2018/04/16/ufos-natural-explanations/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: prologue.blogs.archives.gov">[Pieces of History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">prologue.blogs.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pieces of History UFOs: Natural Explanations – Pieces of History</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pieces of HistoryUFOs: Natural Explanations – Pieces of HistoryApril 16, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2018</span></span></span>

## Why the Cases Are Not a Uniform Dataset

One of the biggest misconceptions is that all 701 reports share the same evidential value. They do not.

Project Blue Book operated from the late 1940s through the end of the 1960s, during which investigative procedures, staffing, priorities and available technology changed substantially. A case investigated in the early 1950s might have involved different reporting forms, different technical resources and different standards of documentation than one investigated in 1968.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/news/articles/project-blue-book-50th-anniversary" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesPublic Interest in UFOs Persists 50 Years After Project Blue Book Termination &#124; National ArchivesDecember 5, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 5, 2019</span></span></span>

The unresolved files include reports with widely differing characteristics, such as:

* single-witness observations;
* multiple [independent witnesses]({{ 'independent-witnesses/' | relative_url }});

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* military pilot sightings;
* civilian reports;
* radar-only events;
* combined visual and radar observations;
* daytime observations with relatively detailed descriptions;
* brief night-time light sightings with fewer identifying features.

</div>

Some files contain numerous witness statements, maps, weather information and technical correspondence. Others consist of only a few pages. Remaining unresolved therefore says little by itself about the strength of any individual case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

## The Different Reasons a Report Could Remain Unresolved

The "unknown" category resulted from several different investigative outcomes rather than one common explanation.

### No Conventional Explanation Fit the Available Evidence

Some reports contained sufficient observational detail to rule out the explanations most commonly examined by investigators, including known aircraft, balloons, astronomical bodies, meteors or weather effects. Rather than forcing an uncertain identification, Blue Book sometimes retained the "unidentified" classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://prologue.blogs.archives.gov/2018/04/16/ufos-natural-explanations/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: prologue.blogs.archives.gov">[Pieces of History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">prologue.blogs.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pieces of History UFOs: Natural Explanations – Pieces of History</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pieces of HistoryUFOs: Natural Explanations – Pieces of HistoryApril 16, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2018</span></span></span>

### Competing Explanations Were Equally Plausible

Certain reports contained evidence pointing in different directions. A witness description might resemble an aircraft while the reported manoeuvres or timing conflicted with available flight information. In these situations investigators occasionally concluded that no single explanation could be supported with confidence.

### Investigation Reached Practical Limits

Blue Book had finite staff, incomplete historical records and limited access to every possible source of corroboration. Civilian flight information, classified military activities and local environmental conditions were not always fully reconstructable years later. Some cases therefore remained unresolved because investigators exhausted the evidence available to them rather than because the observations necessarily represented something extraordinary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/news/articles/project-blue-book-50th-anniversary" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesPublic Interest in UFOs Persists 50 Years After Project Blue Book Termination &#124; National ArchivesDecember 5, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 5, 2019</span></span></span>

### Scientific Uncertainty

Many observations involved transient atmospheric or optical phenomena that were difficult to reproduce or verify. Even when investigators suspected a natural explanation, the available evidence sometimes fell short of proving it conclusively.

## What the 701 Cases Do—and Do Not—Represent

The unresolved category has often been interpreted in two opposing ways that both oversimplify the evidence.

One interpretation treats every unknown as proof of an extraordinary phenomenon. The official Air Force position rejected that conclusion, stating that none of the unidentified cases demonstrated a threat to national security, evidence of technology beyond contemporary scientific understanding or evidence of extraterrestrial vehicles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.secretsdeclassified.af.mil/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/459832/project-blue-book/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: secretsdeclassified.af.mil">[Secrets Declassified]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">secretsdeclassified.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

The opposite interpretation assumes that every unresolved report would inevitably be explained if more information were available. That conclusion also goes beyond the historical record. Blue Book closed with 701 cases still lacking satisfactory conventional identifications, and no comprehensive official [reclassification]({{ 'reclassification/' | relative_url }}) programme later eliminated them as a group.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.secretsdeclassified.af.mil/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/459832/project-blue-book/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: secretsdeclassified.af.mil">[Secrets Declassified]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">secretsdeclassified.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

The most evidence-based interpretation is narrower: the 701 cases identify where the Air Force's investigative process reached its limits using the information, methods and resources available at the time.

## Why the Dataset Remains Valuable

For historians and researchers studying [UFO report databases]({{ 'ufo-report-databases/' | relative_url }}), the significance of the 701 cases lies less in what they prove than in what they preserve.

Because the complete Blue Book archive has been transferred to the U.S. National Archives and extensively digitised, researchers can examine original questionnaires, witness statements, sketches, photographs, correspondence and investigative notes instead of relying only on published summaries. This allows individual cases to be reassessed using modern historical methods, improved astronomical software, weather reconstructions and digitised aviation records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/catalog/catalog-bulk-downloads/uap-bulk-download" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on archives.gov.</span></span></span>

The unresolved cases therefore function as a documented archive of investigations that ended without satisfactory resolution—not as a homogeneous body of evidence pointing to a single cause. Their enduring importance is as a residual dataset showing where one of the largest official UFO investigations in history could not confidently explain every report, while also illustrating the practical limits of mid-twentieth-century investigative procedures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/19" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/19 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>
