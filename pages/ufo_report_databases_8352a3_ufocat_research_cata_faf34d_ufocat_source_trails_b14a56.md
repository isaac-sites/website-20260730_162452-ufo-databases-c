## Introduction

[UFOCAT]({{ 'ufocat/' | relative_url }}) is most useful when treated as a guide back to earlier evidence rather than as the final record of a UFO sighting. Instead of asking only *what happened*, the catalogue also records *where this version of the story came from*. By preserving both direct source information and credited or indirect source information, UFOCAT allows researchers to reconstruct the path a report followed as it moved from witness statements to investigation files, magazines, books and later catalogues. That source trail often reveals how an account evolved, which details were added or omitted, and whether later writers relied on original documentation or merely repeated earlier publications. CUFOS describes UFOCAT as a bibliographic reference designed to lead researchers towards the underlying records rather than replace them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/cufos-publications-databases/ufocat/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies UFOCAT</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesUFOCAT - Center for UFO Studies...</span></span></span>

## Can UFOCAT Lead Back to the Original Report?

In many cases, yes—but only by following several linked references rather than expecting a single definitive record.

A typical UFO report may have been investigated by a civilian organisation, summarised in a newsletter, expanded in a book years later and eventually entered into several catalogues. UFOCAT often contains separate entries representing these different appearances. Rather than treating them as independent sightings, the database records enough source information to help identify that they refer to the same event.

This backwards approach differs from a conventional incident database. Instead of stopping at the latest published version, researchers can work from a modern compilation back through progressively earlier documentation until they reach the closest surviving record to the witness or field investigator. Where original files still exist, this may lead to interview notes, witness questionnaires, correspondence or investigator reports. Where the original material has been lost, the trail at least shows the earliest surviving publication and the chain of authors who relied upon it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/cufos-publications-databases/ufocat/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies UFOCAT</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesUFOCAT - Center for UFO Studies...</span></span></span>

## Direct Sources versus Credited Sources

The mechanism depends largely on two complementary source fields.

The **direct source** identifies the document or archive from which the UFOCAT record itself was entered. This is the publication, investigation file or collection that supplied the catalogue entry.

The **indirect source** (stored in the ISOURCE field) records the source credited by that publication's author. In other words, it answers the question: *where did the author say this information came from?* According to the UFOCAT codebook, this field serves two principal purposes:

* helping group records that refer to the same event;
* identifying primary entries and indicating how close they are to original witness material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/UFOCAT%20Codebook%202023.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies THE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USER&#x27;S GUIDE UFOCAT 2023 Concord, New Ham</span></span></span>

The codebook also specifies conventions for recording different kinds of credited sources. For example:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* an investigator's written field report receives a special priority code;
* a completed witness report form is distinguished from a later narrative;
* witness letters and unstructured interviews receive separate indicators;
* newspaper accounts are marked differently from formal investigation records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/UFOCAT%20Codebook%202023.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies THE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USER&#x27;S GUIDE UFOCAT 2023 Concord, New Ham</span></span></span>

</div>

These distinctions matter because two records may describe the same incident while resting on very different evidential foundations. A magazine article based on a newspaper story carries a longer chain of transmission than an investigator's original interview notes, even if both ultimately describe the same sighting.

## Reading Page, Date and File-Location Fields

Source tracing rarely depends on one field alone. Researchers normally combine several pieces of bibliographic information.

Useful fields typically include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Source abbreviation**, identifying the publication or archive.
* **Author information**, helping distinguish between multiple works by the same researcher.
* **Publication date**, showing whether an account is contemporary or retrospective.
* **Volume and page references**, allowing the original printed discussion to be located.
* **File or collection identifiers**, where available, pointing towards archived investigation material rather than published summaries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/UFOCAT%20Codebook%202023.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies THE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USER&#x27;S GUIDE UFOCAT 2023 Concord, New Ham</span></span></span>

</div>

Taken together, these details allow researchers to reconstruct a citation much as a historian would follow footnotes through successive publications.

For example, a book published in the 1990s may cite an article from a UFO journal published in the 1970s, which itself credits an investigator's report prepared shortly after the event. UFOCAT's fields make that chain visible, allowing the reader to move step by step towards the earliest surviving documentation instead of accepting the newest summary at face value.

## Building a Source Trail from Books to Witnesses

A typical reconstruction follows a sequence rather than a single lookup.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/ufo_report_databases_8352a3_ufocat_research_cata_faf34d_ufocat_source_trails_b14a56-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Source Trails illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/ufo_report_databases_8352a3_ufocat_research_cata_faf34d_ufocat_source_trails_b14a56-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/ufo_report_databases_8352a3_ufocat_research_cata_faf34d_ufocat_source_trails_b14a56-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">

1. Begin with the UFOCAT entry describing the version of interest.
2. Identify its direct source—the publication or archive used to create the catalogue record.
3. Examine the credited indirect source recorded by the author.
4. Locate that earlier publication or investigation file.
5. Repeat the process until reaching the earliest available witness document or investigator record. [Center for UFO Studies](https://cufos.org/PDFs/UFOCAT%20Codebook%202023.pdf)

Not every chain reaches an original witness interview. Older investigations may have been discarded, private files may remain inaccessible, or publications may simply cite "an investigator" without precise references. The codebook explicitly notes that indirect source identifiers are not always unique because authors sometimes provide incomplete citations. Even so, recording imperfect references is preferable to losing the chain altogether, since it preserves clues for later archival research. [Center for UFO Studies](https://cufos.org/PDFs/UFOCAT%20Codebook%202023.pdf)

## Why Source Trails Matter

Following a report backwards often changes how a case is interpreted.

A dramatic claim appearing in a later book may prove to be a condensed version of a much longer interview in which uncertainty, alternative explanations or contradictory witness statements were discussed. Conversely, a brief newspaper article may lead to a detailed investigator's file containing sketches, timings and follow-up interviews that were never published.

The source trail also helps distinguish between genuinely independent evidence and repeated retellings. If several books all cite the same investigation file, they do not represent multiple independent confirmations of the event. Instead, they document the influence of one original source. Recognising this prevents researchers from mistakenly treating repeated publications as repeated observations.

For this reason, UFOCAT functions less as a catalogue of isolated sightings than as a map of documentary relationships. Its greatest strength is not deciding which reports are true, but showing how published accounts connect to earlier records and helping researchers locate the closest surviving evidence to the original witness testimony. [Center for UFO Studies](https://cufos.org/cufos-publications-databases/ufocat/)
