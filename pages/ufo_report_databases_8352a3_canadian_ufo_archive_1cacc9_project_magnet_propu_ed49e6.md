## Introduction

Project Magnet was not an attempt by the Canadian government to prove that flying saucers were extraterrestrial craft. Instead, it was a small Department of Transport research programme that asked a more limited question: if some unexplained aerial reports reflected a genuine physical phenomenon, could that phenomenon be measured and perhaps even lead to new ideas about propulsion? Between 1950 and 1954, senior radio engineer Wilbert B. Smith used existing government facilities, scientific instruments and a dedicated observation station to test whether unusual magnetic or electromagnetic effects accompanied reported UFO activity. The project produced no reproducible evidence supporting Smith's propulsion theory, and official backing ended after several years of inconclusive results and growing concern over the project's increasingly speculative public profile.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/research-help/science-technology/ufos.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[canada.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">s UFOs: The search for the unknown</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canada&#x27;s UFOs: The search for the unknown - Canada.caMarch 2, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 2, 2026</span></span></span>

## Wilbert Smith's theory and government approval

Project Magnet originated with Wilbert Brockhouse Smith, a respected Department of Transport engineer whose professional work centred on radio communications and measurements. Influenced by the rapid growth of flying saucer reports after 1947, Smith proposed that at least some unexplained sightings might involve technology exploiting the Earth's magnetic field. Rather than beginning with the assumption of extraterrestrial visitors, his proposal framed the subject as a potential problem in applied physics and geomagnetism. If such a propulsion method existed, understanding it could have significant scientific and engineering value.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://declassified.ca/project-magnet/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: declassified.ca">[declassified.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">declassified.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Magnet</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Magnet - DECLASSIFIED...</span></span></span>

The Department of Transport authorised Project Magnet in late 1950 as a modest, part-time investigation that could use existing departmental resources rather than a large dedicated budget. This distinction is important. The government approved research into Smith's hypothesis, but it did not officially endorse his conclusions about UFOs. Project Magnet remained a small experimental initiative largely identified with Smith himself rather than with Canadian government policy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.experiencersupport.org/declassified/project-magnet-a-chronology-of-ufo-sightings-and-investigations" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: experiencersupport.org">[experiencersupport.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">experiencersupport.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">project magnet a chronology of ufo sightings and investigations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Magnet: A Chronology of UFO Sightings and Investigations (1950-1952) &#124; The Experiencer Support Association...</span></span></span>

Smith's research programme focused on whether reported UFOs produced measurable physical signatures rather than simply collecting witness testimony. His working assumption was that any advanced propulsion system manipulating magnetic forces should leave detectable traces, making instrumentation more valuable than anecdotal reports alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.experiencersupport.org/declassified/project-magnet-a-chronology-of-ufo-sightings-and-investigations" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: experiencersupport.org">[experiencersupport.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">experiencersupport.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">project magnet a chronology of ufo sightings and investigations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Magnet: A Chronology of UFO Sightings and Investigations (1950-1952) &#124; The Experiencer Support Association...</span></span></span>

## How Project Magnet tried to test propulsion claims

Unlike many contemporary UFO investigations, Project Magnet attempted to move beyond interviews and sighting reports by designing observational tests.

Smith's approach included several complementary methods:

* **Instrument monitoring:** Equipment such as magnetometers, radio receivers, radiation detectors and gravimetric instruments was assembled to look for unusual physical effects that might coincide with reported aerial phenomena.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mysteriesofcanada.com/canada/wilbert-smith/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mysteriesofcanada.com">[mysteriesofcanada.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mysteriesofcanada.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Magnet – Wilbert Smith – Canadian UFO Researcher</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Magnet – Wilbert Smith – Canadian UFO Researcher</span></span></span>
* **Correlation of observations:** Smith compared instrument readings with visual reports in the hope that independently measured anomalies would support eyewitness accounts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.experiencersupport.org/declassified/project-magnet-a-chronology-of-ufo-sightings-and-investigations" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: experiencersupport.org">[experiencersupport.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">experiencersupport.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">project magnet a chronology of ufo sightings and investigations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Magnet: A Chronology of UFO Sightings and Investigations (1950-1952) &#124; The Experiencer Support Association...</span></span></span>
* **Testing witness reliability:** In one experiment, a balloon was deliberately launched over Ottawa to observe how accurately members of the public and the media described an unexpected object in the sky. The exercise demonstrated how easily conventional objects could generate varied UFO reports, highlighting the importance of controlled observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/sites/default/files/documents/report-sky-canada-project.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.ised-isde.canada.ca">[science.ised-isde.canada.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report sky canada project</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">...</span></span></span>

These experiments reflected an unusual mixture of conventional scientific practice and speculative theory. On one hand, Smith emphasised measurements, repeatability and instrumentation. On the other, the underlying hypothesis—that advanced craft might exploit geomagnetic forces for propulsion—had little support from established physics and was never experimentally confirmed.

## The Shirley's Bay observation station

Project Magnet's best-known facility was the observation station established at Shirley's Bay, west of Ottawa. Located on government property where scientific and defence research already occurred, the site offered relatively dark skies, restricted public access and space for specialised equipment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mysteriesofcanada.com/canada/wilbert-smith/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mysteriesofcanada.com">[mysteriesofcanada.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mysteriesofcanada.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Magnet – Wilbert Smith – Canadian UFO Researcher</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Magnet – Wilbert Smith – Canadian UFO Researcher</span></span></span>

The station served as an experimental observatory rather than a routine UFO reporting office. Smith equipped it with instruments intended to detect radio disturbances, magnetic fluctuations and other unusual environmental effects while observers simultaneously watched the sky. The underlying idea was straightforward: if an unconventional propulsion system interacted with the Earth's magnetic field, instruments might record objective evidence even when photographs or eyewitness accounts remained ambiguous.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mysteriesofcanada.com/canada/wilbert-smith/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mysteriesofcanada.com">[mysteriesofcanada.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mysteriesofcanada.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Magnet – Wilbert Smith – Canadian UFO Researcher</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Magnet – Wilbert Smith – Canadian UFO Researcher</span></span></span>

Some later accounts describe isolated anomalous readings recorded at Shirley's Bay, particularly during 1954. However, no observations from the station produced repeatable, independently verified evidence demonstrating a novel propulsion mechanism. Contemporary official records and later historical reviews consistently characterise the experimental results as inconclusive rather than confirmatory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/sites/default/files/documents/report-sky-canada-project.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.ised-isde.canada.ca">[canada.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report sky canada project</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">...</span></span></span>

## Why official support ended in 1954

Several factors combined to end Project Magnet.(#endnote-18 "Endnote 18")<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://majesticdocuments.com/investigations/official/project-magnet/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: majesticdocuments.com">[majesticdocuments.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">majesticdocuments.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Magnet &#124; Majestic Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Magnet &#124; Majestic Documents</span></span></span>

First, the experiments failed to generate reproducible scientific evidence supporting Smith's central hypothesis. Despite several years of observations, the programme did not produce results that convinced other government scientists that UFO propulsion had been experimentally demonstrated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/sites/default/files/documents/report-sky-canada-project.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.ised-isde.canada.ca">[science.ised-isde.canada.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report sky canada project</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">...</span></span></span>

Second, Smith's public statements increasingly moved beyond cautious investigation. By the early 1950s he had become personally convinced that at least some UFOs were extraterrestrial vehicles using advanced technology. Those views attracted media attention, particularly after American UFO author Donald Keyhoe publicised Project Magnet, making it more difficult for the Department of Transport to present the programme as a narrowly focused scientific investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canadaufohistory.com/1947-1954/project-magnet" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canadaufohistory.com">[canadaufohistory.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canadaufohistory.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canada UFO HistoryCanada UFO History - Project Magnet...</span></span></span>

## What Project Magnet contributed to Canada's UFO archives

Although Project Magnet failed to validate its propulsion hypothesis, it occupies a distinctive place within Canada's government UFO records.

Its significance lies in demonstrating that Canadian officials briefly attempted to investigate UFO claims through physical measurement rather than relying solely on witness interviews or intelligence assessments. The surviving correspondence, technical memoranda and reports show how one government engineer sought to convert extraordinary claims into experimentally testable questions, even if the proposed mechanisms ultimately lacked empirical support.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/research-help/science-technology/ufos.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[canada.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">s UFOs: The search for the unknown</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canada&#x27;s UFOs: The search for the unknown - Canada.caMarch 2, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 2, 2026</span></span></span>

The archived files also illustrate an important distinction that is often lost in later retellings. Government authorisation of Project Magnet did not represent official acceptance of extraterrestrial spacecraft or revolutionary propulsion systems. Instead, it reflected a willingness to examine an unusual hypothesis during the early Cold War using limited resources. When the experiments failed to produce convincing evidence, institutional support was withdrawn.

Within the broader Canadian Government UFO files and archives, Project Magnet therefore serves less as evidence that officials proved extraordinary propulsion claims than as a documented example of how a government department tested an unconventional idea, recorded its methods and results, and ultimately concluded that the available evidence did not justify continuing the programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/research-help/science-technology/ufos.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[canada.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">s UFOs: The search for the unknown</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canada&#x27;s UFOs: The search for the unknown - Canada.caMarch 2, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 2, 2026</span></span></span>
