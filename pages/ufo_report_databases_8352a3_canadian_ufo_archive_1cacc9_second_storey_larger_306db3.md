## Introduction

Project Second Storey was Canada's principal interdepartmental effort to determine whether [unidentified]({{ 'unidentified/' | relative_url }}) flying object (UFO) reports justified a permanent, large-scale government investigation. Rather than becoming an expanding research programme, the committee reached the opposite conclusion. After reviewing available reports, developing reporting procedures and assessing both scientific and security implications, it determined that the evidence did not justify a broader national inquiry. The committee's work between 1952 and 1954 shaped Canada's official approach for years afterwards: maintain records, investigate only exceptional cases where genuine scientific or defence value might exist, but avoid creating a dedicated national UFO investigation programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/sites/default/files/documents/report-sky-canada-project.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.ised-isde.canada.ca">[science.ised-isde.canada.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report sky canada project</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2026</span></span></span>

## How the interdepartmental committee worked

Project Second Storey was established in April 1952 under the Defence Research Board, Canada's military science organisation within the Department of National Defence. It was chaired by astronomer Dr Peter Millman and included representatives from military intelligence, the armed services and other government departments. The committee's purpose was not to prove or disprove extraordinary claims but to advise whether UFO reports warranted a sustained governmental programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/sites/default/files/documents/report-sky-canada-project.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.ised-isde.canada.ca">[science.ised-isde.canada.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report sky canada project</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2026</span></span></span>

Rather than conducting extensive field investigations itself, the committee concentrated on improving how reports were collected and evaluated. Members examined existing sighting reports, discussed interviewing techniques, considered methods for cataloguing cases and drafted a standard reporting form intended to make information submitted by different departments more consistent. This reflected a practical administrative goal: before deciding whether a major investigation was necessary, officials wanted to know whether reliable evidence could be gathered in a systematic way.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://condon.ncas.org/text/s5chap03.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: condon.ncas.org">[ncas.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">condon.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Condon Report, Sec V, Chap 3 -- UFO Study Progs in Foreign Countries</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Condon Report, Sec V, Chap 3 -- UFO Study Progs in Foreign Countries</span></span></span>

The committee therefore functioned primarily as an advisory and coordinating body. Operational collection of reports remained with military and other government organisations that already encountered unusual aerial observations during their normal duties.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://condon.ncas.org/text/s5chap03.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: condon.ncas.org">[condon.ncas.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">condon.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Condon Report, Sec V, Chap 3 -- UFO Study Progs in Foreign Countries</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Condon Report, Sec V, Chap 3 -- UFO Study Progs in Foreign Countries</span></span></span>

## What officials hoped classification could reveal

One reason for establishing common reporting procedures was the expectation that careful classification might expose useful patterns hidden within individual reports.

Officials hoped that standardised records could help distinguish between:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* astronomical objects such as planets and meteors;
* atmospheric or meteorological phenomena;
* aircraft, balloons and other conventional objects;
* reports potentially relevant to Canadian air defence;
* genuinely unexplained observations deserving closer examination.

</div>

The underlying assumption was that a sufficiently large and well-organised dataset would reveal whether UFO reports represented a coherent phenomenon or simply a mixture of unrelated events. In practice, the committee found no evidence that classification uncovered a distinct class of incidents requiring a specialised government organisation. Instead, most reports could either be explained, lacked sufficient information for analysis or offered little scientific value.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/sites/default/files/documents/report-sky-canada-project.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.ised-isde.canada.ca">[science.ised-isde.canada.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report sky canada project</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2026</span></span></span>

The committee's emphasis on standardisation also reflected a broader scientific mindset. Millman later supported the idea of internationally consistent reporting formats, arguing that comparable records were more valuable than isolated anecdotes. However, this preference for better documentation did not translate into support for a permanent Canadian investigative programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://condon.ncas.org/text/s5chap03.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: condon.ncas.org">[condon.ncas.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">condon.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Condon Report, Sec V, Chap 3 -- UFO Study Progs in Foreign Countries</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Condon Report, Sec V, Chap 3 -- UFO Study Progs in Foreign Countries</span></span></span>

## Why officials rejected a larger investigation

Several considerations combined to persuade the committee that a major inquiry was unnecessary.

First, the available evidence did not indicate a national security threat. During the early Cold War, any unidentified object in Canadian airspace had to be considered in the context of defence, but the committee found no persuasive indication that reported sightings represented hostile aircraft or other military dangers requiring a dedicated investigative organisation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/sites/default/files/documents/report-sky-canada-project.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.ised-isde.canada.ca">[science.ised-isde.canada.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report sky canada project</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2026</span></span></span>

Second, officials saw limited scientific return. Although some cases remained unexplained because of incomplete information, unresolved did not automatically mean extraordinary. Reports generally lacked the quality, consistency or measurable data needed to support sustained scientific research. The committee therefore concluded that large-scale investment would probably produce little additional knowledge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/sites/default/files/documents/report-sky-canada-project.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.ised-isde.canada.ca">[science.ised-isde.canada.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report sky canada project</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2026</span></span></span>

Third, the committee distinguished its cautious institutional assessment from the more speculative views associated with Wilbert B. Smith's [Project Magnet]({{ 'project-magnet/' | relative_url }}). Smith explored unconventional ideas about UFO propulsion and geomagnetism, but those views were regarded as his own rather than official Canadian government policy. Project Second Storey evaluated the broader evidence independently and did not endorse Smith's conclusions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://condon.ncas.org/text/s5chap03.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: condon.ncas.org">[ncas.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">condon.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Condon Report, Sec V, Chap 3 -- UFO Study Progs in Foreign Countries</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Condon Report, Sec V, Chap 3 -- UFO Study Progs in Foreign Countries</span></span></span>

Finally, officials judged that existing departments could continue handling reports without establishing a permanent specialist organisation. ## Why the committee closed in 1954

Project Second Storey held only a small number of meetings over roughly two years before becoming inactive. Its closure reflected completion of its advisory role rather than the resolution of every UFO report.

By 1954, the committee had reached its central policy recommendation:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* no evidence justified a large-scale official investigation;
* reporting procedures had been developed;
* responsibility for maintaining files could remain within existing government structures;
* exceptional cases could still receive attention when they appeared to have genuine scientific or operational significance.

</div>

The standard reporting form produced by the committee was never widely adopted across government, illustrating the limited institutional momentum behind creating a permanent UFO bureaucracy. Meanwhile, Project Magnet also ended in 1954, further reducing official enthusiasm for expanding Canadian UFO research programmes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/sites/default/files/documents/report-sky-canada-project.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.ised-isde.canada.ca">[canada.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report sky canada project</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2026</span></span></span>

## Lasting influence on Canadian UFO archives

Although Project Second Storey rejected a larger inquiry, its influence persisted in the way Canada handled UFO reports.

Rather than creating a permanent investigative agency, Canada adopted a record-keeping model. Departments continued receiving reports through existing administrative channels, maintaining central files while reserving detailed investigation for unusual cases with possible scientific or defence significance. This philosophy later informed the management of Canada's "Non-Meteoritic Sightings" files and, eventually, the archival collections preserved by Library and Archives Canada.

For researchers examining Canadian government UFO files today, Project Second Storey is significant not because it uncovered dramatic evidence but because it established the administrative principle that shaped the country's official response: collect information, standardise reporting where practical and evaluate reports selectively, while concluding that the available evidence did not warrant a permanent national UFO investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://condon.ncas.org/text/s5chap03.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: condon.ncas.org">[condon.ncas.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">condon.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Condon Report, Sec V, Chap 3 -- UFO Study Progs in Foreign Countries</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Condon Report, Sec V, Chap 3 -- UFO Study Progs in Foreign Countries</span></span></span>
