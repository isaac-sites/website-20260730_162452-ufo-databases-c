## Introduction

A rise in the number of UFO or [unidentified]({{ 'unidentified/' | relative_url }}) anomalous phenomena (UAP) reports does not necessarily mean that more unusual events are occurring. One of the clearest non-astronomical explanations for growing database totals is the creation of reporting channels that reduce the personal, professional or social cost of coming forward. When witnesses believe they can report confidentially, avoid ridicule or receive a fair hearing, people who previously remained silent may begin submitting accounts that describe events from both the present and the past. The result is a larger catalogue of cases without any corresponding increase in the number of objects actually observed. NASA has identified stigma as a major cause of data loss, while recent research on UAP witnesses suggests that only a minority ever report their experiences to an official body or specialist organisation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.narcap.org/faa-reporting-recommendation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: narcap.org">[narcap.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">narcap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Platte, John-Michael Guttierez, Ted Roe, Ryan Graves, 2023 — National Aviation Reporting Center on Anomalous PhenomenaJune 14, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 14, 2023</span></span></span>

## How Stigma Suppresses Witness Participation

Every reporting system reflects not only what people observe but also what they are willing to disclose. For decades, UFO reporting has carried a reputation risk that distinguishes it from reporting many other unusual events. Witnesses have described concerns about being labelled unreliable, attracting unwanted publicity or damaging professional credibility.

These pressures can be especially important for people whose careers depend on perceptions of judgement and competence. Pilots, military personnel, scientists and law enforcement officers may have stronger incentives to stay silent than members of the general public, even if they regard an observation as genuine or operationally significant. The existence of this "hidden population" means public databases inevitably miss some proportion of sightings.

Research supports this interpretation. A survey examining self-reported UAP experiences found that only around 28% of respondents who claimed to have witnessed a phenomenon had reported it to any organisation, while only 14% had contacted a specialist UFO reporting organisation. ## What Changes When Anonymity Is Protected

Reducing stigma changes witness behaviour rather than the underlying phenomenon. Confidential or protected reporting systems remove barriers that previously filtered cases out before they reached a database.

Several mechanisms can increase case numbers:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* **Reduced reputational risk.** Witnesses who feared ridicule become more willing to submit accounts.
* **Professional protection.** Pilots, government personnel and other professionals may report events they previously discussed only privately.
* **Delayed disclosure.** Individuals sometimes report historical experiences years after they occurred once they believe reporting has become acceptable.
* **Improved institutional trust.** Witnesses are more likely to contribute when they believe reports will be evaluated seriously rather than dismissed automatically.

</div>

Importantly, these additional reports may describe events spanning many years. A sudden increase in database entries can therefore reflect the release of previously suppressed testimony rather than a sudden wave of new sightings.

## Aviation Reporting Illustrates the Principle

The aviation community provides one of the clearest examples of how confidentiality and reduced stigma affect reporting behaviour.

The National Aviation Reporting Center on Anomalous Phenomena (NARCAP) explicitly modelled aspects of its reporting process on NASA's Aviation Safety Reporting System (ASRS), emphasising confidentiality to encourage pilots, air traffic controllers and airport personnel to submit incidents without fear of professional repercussions. NARCAP argues that longstanding cultural bias has discouraged reporting despite potential aviation safety implications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.narcap.org/faa-reporting-recommendation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: narcap.org">[narcap.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">narcap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Platte, John-Michael Guttierez, Ted Roe, Ryan Graves, 2023 — National Aviation Reporting Center on Anomalous PhenomenaJune 14, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 14, 2023</span></span></span>

Recent official reporting pathways show a similar awareness of these barriers. The U.S. Department of Defense's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) provides mechanisms for eligible government personnel to submit protected information and highlights protections against reprisal for authorised reports. While operational reporting still follows established military and aviation procedures, the creation of formal channels reflects recognition that witnesses may be reluctant to come forward without institutional safeguards.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Next-AARO-Home-redesign/Next-Parent/Next-Submit-a-Report-dup/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Submit A Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Submit A Report...</span></span></span>

The lesson for database analysis is straightforward: improved reporting procedures may increase recorded cases even if the underlying rate of unusual observations remains unchanged.

## Separating Newly Reported Testimony from New Events

Researchers analysing UFO databases must distinguish between **when an event occurred** and **when it entered the catalogue**. These dates are often very different.

For example, a witness may observe an unusual object in 2018 but wait until 2025—after public attitudes shift or a trusted reporting channel becomes available—to submit an account. A database recording the report date would show an increase in 2025 despite no corresponding increase in sightings that year.

This distinction is especially important after:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* introduction of confidential reporting systems;
* major government announcements encouraging reporting;
* highly publicised hearings or official investigations;
* media coverage that reduces perceived stigma.

</div>

Without accounting for reporting delay, analysts may incorrectly interpret administrative changes as changes in the external phenomenon.

## Why Larger Databases Are Not Necessarily Evidence of More UFO Activity

From the perspective of database interpretation, safer reporting channels generally improve **completeness**, not necessarily **incidence**.

A larger catalogue may indicate that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* previously silent witnesses are participating;
* historical cases are finally being documented;
* professional observers have greater confidence in reporting;
* organisations have become easier to find or more trusted.

</div>

These are valuable developments because they reduce missing data and broaden the range of available testimony. However, they also complicate trend analysis. An increase in reports following stigma reduction cannot automatically be interpreted as evidence that more unidentified objects appeared in the sky.

For anyone analysing [UFO report databases]({{ 'ufo-report-databases/' | relative_url }}) and catalogues, changes in confidentiality, anonymity and witness protection are therefore best understood as changes in the reporting process itself. They affect how many observations become visible to researchers, making database growth an indicator of reporting behaviour as well as of whatever witnesses actually observed.

    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
