## Introduction

A GEIPAN Class D classification is not simply a label for an unexplained UFO report. It is the outcome of a structured investigative process in which conventional explanations have been systematically tested and found insufficient, while the remaining evidence is judged reliable enough to justify leaving the case officially [unidentified]({{ 'unidentified/' | relative_url }}). This makes Class D a deliberately narrow category: most reports are instead identified (Classes A and B) or judged impossible to assess because of inadequate information ([Class C]({{ 'class-c/' | relative_url }})). Only a small proportion of published GEIPAN investigations remain in Class D after review, reflecting the organisation's emphasis on exhausting ordinary explanations before concluding that a case is genuinely unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## Residual strangeness after testing explanations

The central requirement for Class D is what [GEIPAN]({{ 'geipan/' | relative_url }}) calls **residual strangeness**. Rather than asking whether a witness sincerely believes they saw something unusual, investigators ask a different question: *how much of the reported phenomenon remains unexplained after all plausible known causes have been examined?*<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/412" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Since 2008, GEIPAN has formalised this process through a methodology based on two quantitative concepts:

* **Residual strangeness (E):** the degree to which the observation remains inconsistent with the best available conventional explanation after investigation.
* **Consistency (C):** the quality and reliability of the available evidence supporting the report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/412" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Residual strangeness is not measured by how spectacular a sighting appears. Instead, investigators generate competing explanatory hypotheses—including astronomical objects, aircraft, atmospheric phenomena, perception effects and psychological factors—and assess which explanation best accounts for the observation. The stronger the best conventional explanation, the lower the residual strangeness. Only when no known explanation appears sufficiently probable does the case reach the threshold required for consideration as Class D. GEIPAN describes this as residual strangeness exceeding the point at which "no hypothesis seems appropriate."<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

This approach is designed to distinguish between "currently unexplained" and "never explainable." A Class D designation records that investigators did not find an adequate explanation with the available evidence; it does not constitute evidence for extraterrestrial technology or any specific extraordinary hypothesis. GEIPAN explicitly states that [unexplained cases]({{ 'unexplained-cases/' | relative_url }}) should not automatically be interpreted as alien spacecraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## Consistency of witnesses and records

High residual strangeness alone is insufficient for Class D. GEIPAN also requires that the supporting information be internally consistent and sufficiently robust.

Consistency combines both the **quantity** and **quality** of available information. Investigators consider factors including:

* the number of [independent witnesses]({{ 'independent-witnesses/' | relative_url }});

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the precision of witness statements;
* agreement between different accounts;
* photographs, videos or other physical records where available;
* the overall reliability and coherence of the testimony;
* whether witnesses may have influenced one another.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/412" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

</div>

Importantly, consistency does not mean witnesses must agree perfectly. Instead, investigators assess whether differences are compatible with normal observation from different locations or perspectives while looking for contradictions that undermine reliability.

This requirement explains why some apparently remarkable reports never become Class D. A dramatic account supported only by vague memories, uncertain timing or poor documentation is more likely to be classified as **Class C**, meaning there is insufficient information for a reliable conclusion. GEIPAN therefore separates *absence of evidence* from *evidence of an unresolved phenomenon*.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/412" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## Why strong evidence matters more as strangeness increases

GEIPAN deliberately links residual strangeness and consistency rather than treating them as separate checklists. Its methodology follows a straightforward principle: **the more unusual the remaining phenomenon appears, the stronger the supporting evidence must be before investigators accept that it is genuinely unexplained.**<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

This balance serves several purposes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* It reduces the chance that poor-quality observations are mistaken for unexplained phenomena.
* It discourages assigning Class D simply because investigators cannot reconstruct an event from limited information.
* It makes the unidentified category more conservative and therefore more credible.

</div>

The consequence is that many reports with intriguing features never qualify for Class D because the available evidence is not considered reliable enough to sustain an unexplained conclusion.

## The distinction between D1 and D2

Since the 2008 revision of its methodology, GEIPAN has divided Class D into two subcategories that reflect different degrees of residual strangeness while maintaining the requirement for strong consistency.

### D1: Strange but limited in residual strangeness

A **D1** case remains unexplained after investigation and possesses sufficient consistency, but the level of residual strangeness is relatively moderate. There may still be several conceivable explanations, although none fits the evidence well enough to justify classification as an identified case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

### D2: Very high residual strangeness

A **D2** case combines highly reliable documentation with a particularly high level of unexplained residual strangeness. These represent the most unusual files in GEIPAN's catalogue, not because investigators endorse extraordinary interpretations, but because conventional explanations appear especially inadequate given the available evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The distinction therefore concerns **how much unexplained content remains after investigation**, rather than public interest or media attention.

## Class D is not a permanent verdict

GEIPAN does not regard Class D as immutable. Its methodology explicitly allows for cases to be re-examined whenever new evidence becomes available. Additional witness statements, improved image analysis, newly available aviation or astronomical records, or advances in investigative methods may all justify [reclassification]({{ 'reclassification/' | relative_url }}). D cases are therefore subject to periodic review.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The organisation has also reported revisiting historical unexplained cases, with some ultimately being transferred into explained categories after new information emerged. This reflects GEIPAN's policy that an unexplained classification should persist only while the evidence genuinely supports it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## Why only a small fraction become Class D

The rarity of Class D follows directly from GEIPAN's methodology rather than from the number of unusual reports it receives. Most investigations end in one of three ways:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a convincing explanation is identified (Class A or B);
* the available information proves too incomplete for a reliable conclusion (Class C);
* only a small remainder satisfy both requirements of **high residual strangeness** and **high consistency**, qualifying them for Class D.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

</div>

This two-part threshold is what gives the classification its significance. A Class D case represents not merely something unusual, but a report that has survived systematic testing against conventional explanations while remaining supported by evidence judged sufficiently coherent and reliable to justify continued investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>
