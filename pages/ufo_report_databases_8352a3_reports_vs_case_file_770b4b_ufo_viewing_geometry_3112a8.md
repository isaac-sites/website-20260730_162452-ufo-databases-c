## Introduction

A witness account becomes far more useful when investigators can reconstruct exactly where the observer stood, which direction they were facing and how the reported object moved across the visible sky. This process, known as reconstructing the viewing geometry, transforms a subjective description into a testable model. Instead of asking only, "What did the witness think they saw?", investigators ask, "What could have occupied that exact position in the sky at that exact time?"

Within [UFO report databases]({{ 'ufo-report-databases/' | relative_url }}) and catalogues, viewing geometry is one of the most valuable forms of follow-up investigation because it allows reports to be compared objectively with aircraft tracks, satellite passes, astronomical objects and terrain. Even when a case remains [unidentified]({{ 'unidentified/' | relative_url }}), a well-documented geometric reconstruction makes later reanalysis possible as new data or identification tools become available. NASA's UAP study likewise emphasises that precise location, timing and observational metadata are essential if reports are to support scientific investigation rather than anecdotal description.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science UAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAP - NASA ScienceFebruary 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 23, 2026</span></span></span>

## Fixing the Witness Position and Direction

The first task is to establish the observer's position as accurately as possible. Investigators prefer exact coordinates rather than a town name or postcode because even small changes in location can alter the apparent relationship between a sighting and the horizon.

When possible, investigators revisit the site with the witness. Standing in the original position allows them to determine:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the precise observing location using GPS or mapping software;
* the witness's facing direction (azimuth);
* the estimated angle above the horizon (elevation);
* whether the witness moved during the observation;
* the duration of each stage of the event; and
* whether nearby buildings, trees or hills blocked parts of the sky.

</div>

Direction estimates given during interviews are frequently checked rather than accepted at face value. Witnesses often describe an object as travelling "east" or "over the hill", but a site visit may show that the apparent direction was closer to north-east or that the landmark itself was misidentified.

Many investigation protocols therefore encourage recording measurable quantities such as compass bearing, angular elevation and apparent angular size instead of relying solely on descriptive language. [MUFON]({{ 'mufon/' | relative_url }})'s published investigation guidance, for example, lists azimuth, elevation, angular size and other measurable observational parameters among the information collected during follow-up interviews.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mufon.com/the-scientific-method/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mufon.com">[MUFON]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Scientific Method</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Scientific Method - MUFON...</span></span></span>

## Mapping the Visible Sky and Horizon

Once the observer's location is fixed, investigators reconstruct the sky exactly as it appeared at the reported time.

This reconstruction combines several independent sources of information:

* astronomical software showing the positions of planets, bright stars and the Moon;
* topographic maps identifying hills, ridgelines and valleys;
* aerial imagery revealing buildings or vegetation;
* historical [weather records]({{ 'weather-records/' | relative_url }}) indicating cloud cover and visibility; and
* photographs taken from the witness location to document the visible horizon.

The horizon matters because many common misidentifications occur close to it. Bright planets such as Venus can appear unusually large through atmospheric distortion, while aircraft approaching directly towards an observer may seem stationary before changing apparent direction. A witness's memory of an object's height above the horizon may therefore be more informative than their estimate of its distance.

Investigators often express an object's position in angular terms. Holding an arm fully extended provides rough field estimates: a clenched fist spans approximately 10 degrees of sky, while a finger width covers roughly one degree. Although imprecise, such estimates can later be refined against photographs or site measurements.

## Comparing Reconstructed Paths with Known Objects

The reconstructed geometry is then compared with independent records to determine whether known objects occupied the same part of the sky.

Typical comparisons include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* **Aircraft:** Flight-tracking records can establish whether commercial or private aircraft followed the reported path. Navigation lights viewed head-on may appear nearly stationary before suddenly moving sideways as the aircraft changes relative position.
* **Satellites:** Satellite prediction databases help determine whether a reported moving light matches the timing and direction of a satellite pass or a sequence of Starlink satellites.
* **Astronomical objects:** Bright planets, stars and the Moon are compared with the reconstructed line of sight. Venus, Jupiter and Sirius have all generated numerous UFO reports because of their brightness and atmospheric effects near the horizon.
* **Meteors and fireballs:** Reported rapid streaks can be compared with meteor observations and fireball networks.
* **Weather phenomena:** Balloon launches, illuminated clouds and atmospheric optical effects are evaluated where consistent with the reconstructed geometry.

</div>

Rather than asking whether a description sounds similar, investigators ask whether the known object's calculated position matches the witness's reconstructed line of sight within reasonable uncertainty. If the geometry does not fit, that explanation becomes less convincing regardless of superficial similarity. NASA's public guidance similarly notes that careful consideration of celestial objects, satellites, aircraft and atmospheric phenomena resolves many initially puzzling observations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/skywatching/night-sky-network/identifying-ufos-and-uaps/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Identifying UFOs and UAPs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIdentifying UFOs and UAPs - NASA Science...</span></span></span>

## Why Viewing Geometry Often Changes a Case

Reconstructing viewing geometry frequently alters an initial interpretation because human perception provides poor estimates of distance, speed and size without external reference points.

Several recurring effects illustrate why geometry is so important:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A distant aircraft flying directly towards an observer can appear almost motionless before seeming to accelerate sideways.
* Two unrelated lights separated by many kilometres may appear close together from one viewing angle.
* A satellite fading into Earth's shadow can appear to stop abruptly or disappear instantaneously.
* Bright planets near the horizon may seem to hover over trees or buildings despite being astronomically distant.
* A witness turning their head while tracking an object can unintentionally exaggerate apparent motion.

</div>

By reconstructing the observer's viewpoint, investigators can distinguish genuine changes in an object's apparent position from changes caused by perspective or observer movement.

## Limits of Geometric Reconstruction

Viewing geometry is powerful but not infallible. Its accuracy depends on the quality of the original report.

Common limitations include uncertain timing, vague location descriptions, delayed reporting, inaccurate compass directions and imperfect human memory. Even a difference of several minutes can significantly affect comparisons with satellites or fast-moving aircraft. Likewise, if a witness cannot identify where they were standing or which landmarks they used, multiple geometric solutions may remain possible.

Investigators therefore treat reconstructed geometry as an evidence-based model rather than a perfect recreation. Well-documented reports usually include confidence estimates or acknowledge uncertainty in the measured bearings and elevations rather than presenting them as exact values.

## Why Geometry Matters for UFO Databases

For UFO databases and catalogues, viewing geometry adds enduring value because it converts a narrative into structured observational data. Bearings, elevations, observer coordinates and reconstructed paths can be re-examined years later using improved flight records, satellite catalogues or astronomical software that may not have been available during the original investigation.

This makes viewing geometry one of the most important bridges between eyewitness testimony and objective analysis. It does not determine whether a sighting was extraordinary, but it provides the framework needed to test competing explanations consistently, transparently and with evidence that other investigators can independently verify.
