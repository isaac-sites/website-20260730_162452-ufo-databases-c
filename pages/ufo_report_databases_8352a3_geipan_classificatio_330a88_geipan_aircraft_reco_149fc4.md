## Introduction

One of [GEIPAN]({{ 'geipan/' | relative_url }})'s most distinctive investigative practices is that it does not stop when it identifies a likely aircraft. Instead, it asks a second question: **why did an ordinary aircraft appear extraordinary to the witness?** This distinction is central to the French government's approach to investigating [unidentified]({{ 'unidentified/' | relative_url }}) aerospace phenomena. Rather than dismissing a report once radar or flight records identify an aeroplane, investigators reconstruct the exact viewing conditions—observer position, aircraft movement, lighting, terrain and human perception—to explain how a conventional object could genuinely appear stationary, saucer-shaped, triangular or capable of projecting a beam of light. This reconstruction-based method turns a simple identification into a testable explanation and illustrates why many initially dramatic reports ultimately receive an identified or probably identified classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58788" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMethodology &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMethodology &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Access to defence radar is particularly valuable because it allows investigators to determine whether an aircraft occupied precisely the part of the sky described by witnesses. Rather than asking whether *an* aircraft was generally nearby, the investigation asks whether a specific aircraft's position and timing match the observation closely enough to account for it. GEIPAN's agreements with French defence authorities allow it to consult radar traces during appropriate investigations, making this reconstruction considerably more rigorous than relying on publicly available flight data alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## Reconstructing Lighting and Viewing Geometry

Many aircraft cases depend less on the aircraft itself than on the geometry between observer, aircraft and environment.

GEIPAN reconstructs factors including:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The observer's exact position and direction of travel.
* Aircraft heading relative to the witness.
* Moon position and illumination.
* Terrain features such as hills and tree lines.
* Atmospheric visibility.
* Relative motion between observer and aircraft.

</div>

This reconstruction often reveals that several ordinary effects combine into an unexpected visual impression.

One recurring mechanism is the **pivot effect**. When both the observer and the aircraft are moving, the changing line of sight can rotate around a nearly fixed point. To the observer, the aircraft appears almost motionless despite travelling at normal speed. The effect is particularly convincing when viewed from a moving vehicle because the observer naturally judges movement against nearby landscape rather than against distant airspace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Lighting introduces additional distortions. Depending on the angle of illumination, a transport aircraft's rounded fuselage may remain brightly lit while its flatter wings reflect little light towards the observer. The illuminated fuselage can therefore appear as an isolated glowing disc rather than a recognisable aircraft. Terrain can reinforce the illusion: moonlight reflecting from treetops or mist may resemble a downward cone or beam apparently originating from the object itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Rather than treating these effects separately, GEIPAN evaluates how they interact to reproduce the witness's actual description.

## Explaining the C-130 Saucer-like Sighting

GEIPAN's investigation of the **Conches-en-Ouche** sighting on **23 February 2016** has become one of its clearest demonstrations of reconstruction methodology.

Four witnesses observed what they believed was an extraordinary aerial object:

* Three occupants of a moving car described a stationary flying saucer emitting a cone of light.
* A fourth witness, watching from a stationary vehicle, reported an enormous slow-moving triangular object.

Radar records quickly established that a **Lockheed C-130 Hercules** transport aircraft had passed through the area at the relevant time. The challenge was explaining why none of the witnesses recognised it as an aeroplane.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

GEIPAN reconstructed the observation in detail.

For the three moving witnesses:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The pivot effect made the aircraft appear stationary.
* Indirect full-moon illumination reflected diffusely from the aircraft's rounded fuselage while the wings remained comparatively invisible.
* Moonlight illuminating the tops of nearby trees produced a bright halo interpreted as a cone of light beneath the object.

</div>

The combined result closely matched the witnesses' description of a hovering flying saucer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The fourth witness experienced a different illusion despite observing the same aircraft. Because the observer remained stationary, the pivot effect was absent. Instead, GEIPAN concluded that the aircraft's unusually large size, lighting and unfamiliar viewing angle prevented immediate recognition. Investigators also pointed to a perceptual bias in estimating angular speed: larger aircraft often appear to move more slowly because observers unconsciously judge motion by the time taken for the aircraft's tail to occupy the position previously held by its nose. The same physical aircraft therefore generated two distinctly different UFO reports through differing viewing circumstances.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/node/440" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Why Reconstruction Matters More Than Identification Alone

This approach illustrates an important principle in GEIPAN's methodology. Witnesses are not assumed to be dishonest or careless. Instead, investigators distinguish between **accurately reporting what was perceived** and **correctly identifying the external cause**.

An explanation is considered stronger when it accounts simultaneously for:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* The reported shape.
* Apparent motion.
* Brightness.
* Duration.
* Direction of travel.
* Surrounding environmental features.

</div>

Simply naming an aircraft would leave much of the testimony unexplained. By reconstructing lighting, geometry and perception together, GEIPAN attempts to demonstrate how an ordinary object could genuinely produce an extraordinary visual experience. This emphasis on explaining the perceived strangeness, rather than merely assigning a conventional label, is a defining feature of its investigative practice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>
