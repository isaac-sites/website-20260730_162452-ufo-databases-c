## Introduction

GEIPAN’s total number of [unexplained cases]({{ 'unexplained-cases/' | relative_url }}) is not a fixed figure. Unlike many UFO databases that leave historical classifications untouched, France’s official investigation unit periodically revisits older files when new evidence, better analytical methods or improved reference data become available. As a result, the number of cases classified as unexplained ([Class D]({{ 'class-d/' | relative_url }}), now divided into D1 and D2) can fall even if no new reports are received. This approach reflects GEIPAN’s view that an unexplained case represents the best conclusion available at a particular moment rather than a permanent verdict. Official guidance explicitly states that classifications may be revised whenever new information emerges or older investigations are re-examined.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Classification &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Classification &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## The 2008 classification changes made reviews easier

A major step towards more consistent reclassification came with GEIPAN's revised classification procedure introduced in 2008. Instead of relying solely on the traditional A, B, C and D categories, the agency:

* established explicit criteria for assigning classifications;
* distinguished between insufficient evidence ([Class C]({{ 'class-c/' | relative_url }})) and genuinely unexplained observations (Class D);
* divided Class D into D1 (moderately consistent unexplained cases) and D2 (high-consistency unexplained cases).

The purpose was to produce a classification that more accurately reflected the quality of the available evidence rather than simply whether investigators had found an explanation. The revised framework also made later reassessment more systematic because investigators could compare old files against clearly defined criteria.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/index.php/fr/actualites/evolution-classification-des-cas" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">evolution classification des cas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Evolution de la CLASSIFICATION DES CAS &#124; GEIPANDecember 10, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 10, 2008</span></span></span>

## Why the unexplained percentage fell

One of GEIPAN's most notable findings is that the proportion of unexplained cases has declined substantially over time.

In a 2016 analysis, GEIPAN explained that this reduction should not be interpreted as evidence that unusual aerial phenomena themselves became rarer. Instead, several institutional changes affected the statistics:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the number of investigated reports roughly tripled after the organisation expanded its public reporting system and online presence;
* many additional reports involved readily identifiable objects such as sky lanterns, digital imaging artefacts or other commonplace phenomena;
* classification standards became stricter;
* investigations became more thorough through improved digital tools and investigative methods.

</div>

Because the overall number of processed cases increased dramatically while the absolute number of unexplained cases decreased, the percentage of Class D cases fell sharply. GEIPAN reported that the average number of unexplained cases declined from about 6.8 per year between 1975 and 2004 to roughly 2.5 per year after 2005. During the most recent decade examined in that report, unexplained cases represented only about 2% of newly processed files, compared with roughly 10% across the earlier historical dataset.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/actualites/baisse-cas-d" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">baisse cas d</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Forte baisse des CAS D sur les 10 dernières années &#124; GEIPANDecember 14, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 14, 2016</span></span></span>

GEIPAN also cautioned that these figures describe changes in investigation practices and reporting patterns as much as any underlying change in unusual observations themselves.

## The 2017 reclassification of 50 cases

The most visible demonstration of GEIPAN's review policy came during 2017, when investigators completed fresh examinations of approximately 50 older Class D cases that had originally remained unexplained.

According to GEIPAN, these reinvestigations resulted in previously unexplained files being assigned conventional classifications after additional analysis. The work formed part of a broader effort to create a more uniform catalogue of unexplained cases by removing those that no longer satisfied the criteria for Class D under modern standards.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Classification &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Classification &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The review programme illustrates an important feature of the database: historical statistics are not simply accumulated year after year. Instead, they are periodically refined as older cases are reassessed.

## Why "unidentified" is a provisional outcome

A common misunderstanding is that a Class D case represents permanent proof that an event defies explanation. GEIPAN explicitly rejects that interpretation.

Official documentation states that D classifications may be revised whenever:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* new witness testimony is received;
* additional technical evidence becomes available;
* experts identify a previously overlooked explanation;
* historical cases are revisited as part of systematic review programmes.

</div>

Similarly, the organisation notes that D cases require periodic re-evaluation and that reclassification remains possible whenever significant new information appears.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Classification &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Classification &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This makes the unexplained category fundamentally different from a closed historical archive. Instead of treating uncertainty as permanent, GEIPAN regards it as the current state of knowledge after investigation.

## What changing totals mean for readers

Because classifications evolve, readers should avoid comparing unexplained-case totals from different years without considering how the database itself has changed. Three factors influence the published figures simultaneously:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--checklist" markdown="1">

* newly investigated reports enter the database;
* older files may be reassessed and reclassified;
* classification criteria and investigative methods continue to improve.

</div>

Consequently, the number of unexplained cases reflects both the observations being reported and GEIPAN's evolving ability to investigate them. Rather than weakening the database, this willingness to revise earlier conclusions demonstrates that its statistics are intended to represent the best available assessment at any given time, not a permanently fixed catalogue of mysteries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/actualites/baisse-cas-d" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">baisse cas d</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Forte baisse des CAS D sur les 10 dernières années &#124; GEIPANDecember 14, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 14, 2016</span></span></span>
