## Introduction

When analysing the National UFO Reporting Center ([NUFORC]({{ 'nuforc/' | relative_url }})) database, one of the most important methodological decisions is whether to use the **event date** or the **submission (or posting) date**. Although both appear to describe the same report, they answer different questions.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/ufo_report_databases_8352a3_nuforc_report_limits_750bea_ufo_event_vs_submiss_9cae65-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Date Gaps illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/ufo_report_databases_8352a3_nuforc_report_limits_750bea_ufo_event_vs_submiss_9cae65-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/ufo_report_databases_8352a3_nuforc_report_limits_750bea_ufo_event_vs_submiss_9cae65-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

An **event date** estimates when the witness says the sighting occurred. A **submission or posting date** records when the report entered NUFORC's system and became available to researchers. Because NUFORC accepts both recent sightings and recollections from many years earlier, the gap between these dates can range from hours to decades. Researchers who overlook this distinction risk mistaking reporting behaviour or database processing patterns for changes in the underlying frequency of reported sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/databank/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Data Bank &#124; NUFORC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Latest UFO SightingsSeptember 6, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 6, 2021</span></span></span>

## Why event and submission dates diverge

The two dates diverge for several reasons, many of which have little to do with the reported aerial event itself.

First, witnesses often delay reporting. Some submit an account immediately after an experience, while others wait weeks, months or even decades before deciding to contact NUFORC. Personal uncertainty, fear of ridicule, renewed media interest, retirement, or discovering NUFORC long after the event can all encourage retrospective reporting. As a result, an event from the 1980s may first appear in the public database in the 2020s.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/722-new-ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">722 New UFO Reports Posted &#124; NUFORC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">722 New UFO Reports Posted &#124; NUFORC</span></span></span>

Secondly, submission and publication are not identical. After reports are received, NUFORC reviews and processes them before adding them to the public database. The organisation explicitly notes that reports are posted periodically rather than continuously, meaning the public posting date reflects editorial workflow as well as witness behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/databank/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Data Bank &#124; NUFORC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Latest UFO SightingsSeptember 6, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 6, 2021</span></span></span>

Finally, historical reporting practices have changed over time. Before widespread internet access, many reports arrived by telephone or post. Modern [online reporting]({{ 'online-reporting/' | relative_url }}) has reduced delays for many contemporary sightings, but it has also made it easier for people to record memories from much earlier periods, producing a database that mixes contemporary observations with historical recollections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/databank/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Data Bank &#124; NUFORC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Latest UFO SightingsSeptember 6, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 6, 2021</span></span></span>

## How backlogs create artificial spikes

One of the most common analytical mistakes is interpreting a sudden increase in posted reports as evidence that more sightings occurred during that same period.

In practice, large publication batches often combine recent sightings with historical accounts. For example, NUFORC's July 2023 update announced **722 newly posted reports**, explaining that **489 described contemporary sightings while the remainder concerned events from earlier years that witnesses had only recently reported**. The posting date therefore represented a publication event rather than a single surge of sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/722-new-ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">722 New UFO Reports Posted &#124; NUFORC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">722 New UFO Reports Posted &#124; NUFORC</span></span></span>

Similarly, later updates have distinguished between reports from the current reporting period and older sightings submitted retrospectively. A December 2023 update stated that most newly published reports described sightings from that year, while others related to previous years.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/422-new-ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">422 new ufo reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Posted &#124; NUFORCDecember 9, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 9, 2023</span></span></span>

Regular batch publication also creates visible clusters in the posting-date index. A day showing hundreds of newly available reports may simply indicate that staff completed processing accumulated submissions rather than that hundreds of UFO sightings occurred on that day. NUFORC's public database even maintains separate indexes by **event date** and **date posted**, recognising that the two timelines serve different analytical purposes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/ndx/?id=post" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NUFOR C Reports by Date Posted</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC Reports by Date PostedAugust 20, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 20, 2023</span></span></span>

## What each timeline actually measures

Choosing the correct date depends entirely on the research question.

If the research asks...Preferred dateWhen did witnesses report that they observed something unusual?Event dateWhen did reports enter the public record?Submission or posting dateWhen did public interest or reporting activity increase?Submission or posting dateDid a news event coincide with an increase in reports?Compare both timelines

This distinction becomes especially important during periods of heightened public attention. Media coverage, congressional hearings, military incidents or highly visible satellite launches may encourage people both to report new observations and to submit memories of older experiences. A spike in submission dates therefore reflects changing reporting behaviour as much as any change in reported observations themselves.

## Memory and retrospective reporting

Older reports remain valuable, but they should not automatically be treated as equivalent to accounts recorded immediately after an event.

Memory research consistently shows that recollections can change over time. Dates become approximate, durations may be misremembered, and later experiences or cultural narratives can influence how an event is described. This does not mean retrospective reports are necessarily inaccurate or fabricated; rather, they represent a different type of evidence with different strengths and limitations.

For historians, delayed reports may preserve experiences that would otherwise have been lost entirely. For statistical analyses seeking to reconstruct temporal patterns, however, the increasing uncertainty associated with long reporting delays should be recognised and, where possible, measured.

## Best practices for building report timelines

Researchers using NUFORC or derivative datasets can reduce analytical errors by following several straightforward practices.


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* **Separate occurrence from publication.** Never assume the posting date indicates when the sighting happened.
* **Retain both fields during data cleaning.** Removing one date discards information about reporting behaviour.
* **Measure reporting lag.** Calculating the interval between event and submission can reveal periods of delayed reporting or changes in witness behaviour.
* **Identify publication batches.** Large groups of reports posted together should not automatically be interpreted as real-world surges in sightings.
* **Document the chosen timeline.** Any published analysis should clearly state whether charts are based on event dates, submission dates or posting dates.
* **Perform sensitivity checks.** Comparing results under both dating methods helps determine whether observed trends are genuine or artefacts of reporting delays.

</div>

These practices are particularly important when combining NUFORC with other UFO catalogues, since different organisations may record submission, investigation or publication dates differently.

## Why the distinction matters for interpreting UFO databases

The difference between an event date and a submission date is not a minor technical detail but a defining characteristic of UFO reporting databases. Event dates provide the closest available estimate of when witnesses say something occurred, while submission and posting dates document when those experiences became part of the historical record.

Understanding both timelines allows researchers to distinguish between changes in reported observations, changes in public willingness to report, and changes in database processing.
