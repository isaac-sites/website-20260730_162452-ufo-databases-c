## Introduction

[GEIPAN]({{ 'geipan/' | relative_url }})'s published percentages are often misunderstood as measuring how many [unidentified]({{ 'unidentified/' | relative_url }}) objects are flying over France. They do not. Instead, they describe the outcomes of investigations into the subset of reports that have been completed, classified and published in GEIPAN's archive. The figures therefore measure the distribution of investigation results—not the prevalence of unexplained aerial phenomena in French airspace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/stats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANStatistics &#124; GEIPANJune 25, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2026</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/ufo_report_databases_8352a3_geipan_classificatio_330a88_geipan_statistics_me_2e21de-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Case Statistics illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/ufo_report_databases_8352a3_geipan_classificatio_330a88_geipan_statistics_me_2e21de-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/ufo_report_databases_8352a3_geipan_classificatio_330a88_geipan_statistics_me_2e21de-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

This distinction matters because the database combines cases with very different evidential value. Some reports are conclusively identified, others are judged probably identified, many cannot be assessed because too little reliable information exists, and only a small minority remain unexplained after investigation. Understanding what these categories represent is essential before drawing conclusions from GEIPAN's statistics.

## The Balance of Classes A, B, C and D

GEIPAN's public statistics are calculated from classified cases published on its website. As of 25 June 2026, the archive contained 3,368 published classified cases distributed as follows:

* **Class A (28.0%)** – the phenomenon was identified with a high degree of confidence.
* **Class B (38.8%)** – the investigators concluded that a known explanation is the most probable.
* **[Class C]({{ 'class-c/' | relative_url }}) (30.1%)** – the case could not be resolved because the available information was insufficient or unreliable.
* **[Class D]({{ 'class-d/' | relative_url }}) (3.1%)** – the phenomenon remained unidentified after investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/stats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANStatistics &#124; GEIPANJune 25, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2026</span></span></span>

These percentages therefore describe **how investigated reports ended**, not what witnesses originally believed they had seen. A witness may report a spectacular "UFO", yet the investigation may ultimately classify it as A or B after identifying an aircraft, astronomical object or another conventional explanation. Conversely, a report may remain in Class C not because it is mysterious, but because investigators lacked enough dependable evidence to reach any conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Another important point is that GEIPAN's published figures are **dynamic statistics**. Since 2016, they have been calculated from the set of classified cases currently published online. Files still under investigation are excluded until classification, and older cases can be reclassified if new evidence emerges. As a result, the percentages are not fixed historical constants but can change over time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/stats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANStatistics &#124; GEIPANJune 25, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2026</span></span></span>

## Why Report Statistics Are Not Prevalence Estimates

A common mistake is to interpret the approximately 3% of Class D cases as meaning that roughly 3% of all unusual aerial events in France are genuinely unexplained. GEIPAN does not make that claim.

Several reasons explain why such an interpretation is incorrect:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* **The database contains reported events, not every aerial phenomenon.** Many observations are never reported, while others are screened out before becoming published investigation files.
* **The denominator is published classified cases.** The percentages do not refer to all public contacts, all sightings or all events in French airspace.
* **Cases differ enormously in evidential quality.** A detailed multi-witness investigation and a brief report with little supporting information each contribute one classified case.
* **Classification reflects investigative outcome rather than objective rarity.** The figures measure how often investigators reached each type of conclusion under the available evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/stats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANStatistics &#124; GEIPANJune 25, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2026</span></span></span>

</div>

The large proportion of Class C cases illustrates this particularly well. These reports are not counted as "explained", but neither are they counted as genuinely unexplained phenomena. They occupy a separate category precisely because the evidence was inadequate for either conclusion. Treating Class C as evidence for unknown objects would substantially misrepresent what GEIPAN's classifications are intended to convey.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## How Methods and Evidence Quality Shape the Totals

GEIPAN's published statistics are inseparable from its investigative methodology. Classification depends not only on whether investigators can find a plausible explanation but also on the **consistency** and **quality** of the available evidence.

Since 2008, GEIPAN has based classifications on two principal factors:

* **Residual strangeness**—how poorly known explanations account for the reported observation after investigation.
* **Consistency**—the quantity and reliability of the available evidence, including witness statements, documentation, photographs and corroborating information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

This means that a report can remain unresolved for two very different reasons:

* because there is **too little reliable evidence** (Class C); or
* because the evidence is considered sufficiently strong but **no satisfactory conventional explanation has been established** (Class D).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The methodology deliberately prevents these situations from being merged into a single "unknown" category. That separation makes the published statistics more informative, because they distinguish investigative uncertainty from residual [unexplained cases]({{ 'unexplained-cases/' | relative_url }}).

GEIPAN also periodically revisits older files. Improvements in digital tools, accumulated investigative experience and newly available information have led to the [reclassification]({{ 'reclassification/' | relative_url }}) of some historical Class D cases into explained categories. Consequently, the proportion of unexplained cases reflects not only incoming reports but also continuing review of the archive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## What the Published Percentages Can—and Cannot—Support

Properly interpreted, GEIPAN's statistics provide insight into the performance and outcomes of an investigative process rather than evidence about the frequency of extraordinary phenomena.

The figures can legitimately support conclusions such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* most published investigated cases receive an identified or probably identified explanation;
* a substantial fraction of reports remain inconclusive because evidence is insufficient;
* only a small proportion remain unidentified after investigation;
* classifications are provisional in the sense that they can change if new evidence emerges.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/stats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANStatistics &#124; GEIPANJune 25, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2026</span></span></span>

</div>

By contrast, the statistics cannot demonstrate:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* how many unexplained objects actually occur in French skies;
* the probability that any new sighting represents an unknown phenomenon;
* evidence for extraterrestrial technology or any specific hypothesis regarding Class D cases.

</div>
