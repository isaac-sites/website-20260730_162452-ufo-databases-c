## Introduction

A common misunderstanding in discussions of the French government's UFO investigation programme is that any case [GEIPAN]({{ 'geipan/' | relative_url }}) cannot identify is automatically an "unexplained" mystery. That is not how the organisation classifies reports. A **Class C** case does **not** mean investigators found a highly unusual phenomenon that resisted explanation. Instead, it means the available evidence was too limited, incomplete or unreliable to support a meaningful conclusion. By design, GEIPAN separates these poorly documented reports from **[Class D]({{ 'class-d/' | relative_url }})** cases, where investigators conducted a substantive assessment yet still could not reach a satisfactory explanation. This distinction is central to understanding the evidential quality of the GEIPAN database and avoiding the common mistake of treating missing information as evidence of an anomalous event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/412" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## What evidence makes a case Class C?

GEIPAN defines **Class C** as a phenomenon "not identified due to lack of data or information." The emphasis is on the quality of the evidence rather than the apparent strangeness of the reported event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/412" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

A report may receive a Class C classification when investigators cannot establish enough reliable facts about the observation. Typical limitations include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* an incomplete or vague witness account;
* uncertain date, time or location;
* insufficient information about direction, altitude or duration;
* missing photographs, videos or physical evidence where such material would be important;
* contradictory or internally inconsistent testimony;
* inability to reconstruct the viewing conditions because essential details were never recorded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

</div>

In these situations, investigators cannot properly compare the report against ordinary explanations such as aircraft, astronomical objects, atmospheric phenomena or perceptual effects. The classification therefore reflects an evidential limitation rather than a judgement that the phenomenon itself is extraordinary.

## How weak documentation blocks meaningful testing

GEIPAN's methodology is built around two linked concepts:

* **Residual strangeness**, meaning how unusual the observation remains after testing possible explanations.
* **Consistency**, meaning the quantity and reliability of the available evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/412" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Consistency is not simply the number of witnesses. It also includes factors such as the precision of answers, the independence of witnesses, coherence between different accounts, and the availability of supporting material including photographs or videos.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

If consistency is too low, investigators cannot confidently evaluate competing hypotheses. A witness may sincerely describe an extraordinary-looking event, but without enough dependable information there is no sound basis for concluding whether the object was an aircraft, a planet, an atmospheric effect or something genuinely unusual.

GEIPAN therefore treats insufficient evidence as a separate outcome rather than forcing a speculative explanation or declaring the case genuinely unexplained.

## Why Class C should be separated from Class D

The distinction between Class C and Class D is one of the most important features of GEIPAN's classification system.

Class CClass DEvidence is insufficient for meaningful analysis.Evidence is sufficiently consistent to permit a thorough investigation.No reliable conclusion can be reached because essential information is missing.Investigators have examined available hypotheses but no satisfactory explanation remains.Represents an evidential problem.Represents a residual investigative problem after analysis.

GEIPAN explicitly states that stronger claims require stronger evidence. Its methodology follows a simple principle: the more unusual a reported phenomenon appears, the stronger and more consistent the supporting evidence must be before it can legitimately be regarded as unexplained. If that evidential threshold is not met, the observation is classified as Class C rather than Class D.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

This approach reduces the risk of turning poor-quality reports into apparent mysteries.

## Why Class C is not a "hidden mystery" category

Outside the official classification system, lists of "unexplained UFOs" sometimes include every report lacking a conventional identification. GEIPAN deliberately avoids this practice.

A Class C classification does **not** imply:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* investigators exhausted every possible explanation;
* the event demonstrated unknown technology;
* the report was too mysterious to solve;
* the case is evidence for extraterrestrial activity.

</div>

Instead, it means investigators lacked enough dependable information to test explanations properly. An unresolved investigation and an uninvestigable report are treated as fundamentally different outcomes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

This distinction protects the integrity of the database because uncertainty arising from missing evidence is not confused with uncertainty that remains after substantial investigation.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/ufo_report_databases_8352a3_geipan_classificatio_330a88_geipan_class_c_limit_97f2e3-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Class C illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/ufo_report_databases_8352a3_geipan_classificatio_330a88_geipan_class_c_limit_97f2e3-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/ufo_report_databases_8352a3_geipan_classificatio_330a88_geipan_class_c_limit_97f2e3-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">

## Class C cases can change if new evidence appears

A Class C classification is not necessarily permanent. GEIPAN states that both Class C and Class D cases may be revisited if new information becomes available after the original investigation. Additional witness statements, higher-quality images, newly available radar information or other documentary evidence may allow investigators to reassess the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/412" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

This means the classification reflects the evidence available at the time rather than an immutable judgement about the underlying event.

## Why the distinction matters for interpreting the GEIPAN database

When analysing [UFO report databases]({{ 'ufo-report-databases/' | relative_url }}), treating every [unidentified]({{ 'unidentified/' | relative_url }}) report as equally significant creates a misleading picture. GEIPAN's methodology instead separates two different kinds of uncertainty:

* **Insufficient evidence**, where reliable testing is impossible (Class C).
* **Residual unexplained observations**, where investigation has been possible but no satisfactory explanation remains (Class D).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

That separation helps researchers, journalists and readers avoid overstating the number of genuinely anomalous cases. It also explains why approximately one-third of published GEIPAN cases fall into Class C while only a small minority are classified as Class D: many reports simply do not contain enough dependable information to support a defensible conclusion, and GEIPAN's methodology explicitly recognises that "we do not know because the evidence is inadequate" is a different—and often more scientifically honest—answer than "we investigated thoroughly and still cannot explain it."<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>
