## Introduction

[UFOCAT]({{ 'ufocat/' | relative_url }}) is a reference catalogue rather than a census of unique UFO sightings. Its records are designed to preserve the documentary history of an incident by linking together investigation files, magazine articles, books, newspapers and later summaries that describe the same event. As a result, one sighting may legitimately generate several database entries. Counting every row as a separate incident therefore produces inflated totals and can misrepresent both the frequency and the quality of the underlying evidence. The Centre for UFO Studies (CUFOS), which maintains UFOCAT, explicitly warns that simple record counts overstate the number of cases and recommends using the catalogue as a guide to original documentation rather than as a standalone statistical dataset.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/cufos-publications-databases/ufocat/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies UFOCAT</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesUFOCAT - Center for UFO Studies...</span></span></span>

## How incident blocks group related records

The key to understanding UFOCAT is that the basic unit of organisation is not always the individual database row but the group of linked records describing the same reported event.

Each record receives its own permanent identifier, yet it can also be connected to a common primary record representing the best available source for that incident. The codebook explains that the **Primary Record Number (PRN)** points researchers towards the record judged to contain the most primary information. Filtering by that PRN retrieves the associated documentary chain rather than unrelated sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/UFOCAT%20Codebook%202023.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USER&#x27;S GUIDE UFOCAT 2023 Concord, New Ham...</span></span></span>

A single incident might therefore include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* an original investigator's case file;
* a report in a civilian UFO journal;
* coverage in a local newspaper;
* a later chapter in a UFO book;
* a catalogue entry derived from one of those publications.

</div>

These are separate bibliographic records because they preserve different versions or stages of transmission, not because they document different aerial events. This structure allows researchers to compare how an account evolved over time, identify where later authors relied on earlier publications, and locate the closest surviving source to the original witness testimony. CUFOS describes this as one of UFOCAT's principal purposes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/cufos-publications-databases/ufocat/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies UFOCAT</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesUFOCAT - Center for UFO Studies...</span></span></span>

## What the X2 field says about source distance

One of the most important fields for avoiding duplicate counting is **X2**, which the UFOCAT codebook describes as indicating the "handedness" of a record—that is, how close that record is to the original source.

The coding distinguishes several levels of documentary separation:

* **X2 = 0**: the selected primary record for the incident.
* **X2 = 1**: an independent investigation of the same event that was not chosen as the primary record.
* **X2 = 2**: a secondary source that refers to the primary report or another source.
* **X2 = 3**: a source even further removed, relying only on secondary reporting.

Rather than measuring witness reliability, X2 measures the relationship between records within the documentary chain. A magazine article copied from an investigator's report is not treated the same way as the investigation itself, even though both concern the identical sighting. Likewise, two independent investigations of one event may each receive separate records because they contain distinct documentary evidence while still referring to the same occurrence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/UFOCAT%20Codebook%202023.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USER&#x27;S GUIDE UFOCAT 2023 Concord, New Ham...</span></span></span>

This distinction is particularly valuable when tracing citation networks. Researchers can see whether a widely repeated story ultimately depends on one original investigation or whether multiple independent enquiries produced overlapping evidence.

## Why filtered totals still require human review

The UFOCAT documentation notes that filtering for **X2 = 0** provides the standard starting point for statistical work because it removes many duplicate references and leaves only the designated primary entries. CUFOS specifically recommends this approach when researchers need preliminary [case counts]({{ 'case-counts/' | relative_url }}) without obvious over-counting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/UFOCAT%20Codebook%202023.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USER&#x27;S GUIDE UFOCAT 2023 Concord, New Ham...</span></span></span>

Even so, filtering cannot eliminate every ambiguity.

Several factors still require manual assessment:

* **Independent investigations:** More than one investigator may have documented the same event. Although the coding attempts to identify a preferred primary record, complex historical cases sometimes require judgement.
* **Incomplete linkage:** UFOCAT has grown over decades from numerous archives and catalogues. Some relationships between records may remain [unidentified]({{ 'unidentified/' | relative_url }}) or uncertain, especially where original documentation has been lost.
* **Changing source availability:** Newly discovered files or revised historical research can clarify that apparently separate reports actually describe the same incident, or vice versa.
* **Coding uncertainty:** The codebook includes fields expressing the coder's confidence in relationships between linked records, recognising that some documentary connections are probable rather than certain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/UFOCAT%20Codebook%202023.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USER&#x27;S GUIDE UFOCAT 2023 Concord, New Ham...</span></span></span>

For these reasons, even an X2-filtered dataset should be regarded as a starting point for analysis rather than a definitive count of unique sightings.

## Why duplicate records improve, rather than weaken, the catalogue

At first glance, duplicate-looking entries may appear to be a flaw. In practice they are one of UFOCAT's defining research features.

Because UFOCAT functions as a bibliographic reference system, preserving multiple records for the same incident enables researchers to:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* identify the earliest surviving documentation;
* compare later retellings with original investigations;
* determine whether a claim rests on firsthand or derivative reporting;
* locate additional archival material connected with an event.

</div>

The design therefore prioritises documentary transparency over numerical simplicity. Problems arise only when users ignore the catalogue's relational structure and assume that every row represents an independent sighting.
