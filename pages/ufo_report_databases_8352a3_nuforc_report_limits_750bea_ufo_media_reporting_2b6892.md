## Introduction

News coverage can increase the number of UFO reports without any corresponding increase in unusual aerial phenomena. This is one of the most important limitations when interpreting large public databases such as the National UFO Reporting Center ([NUFORC]({{ 'nuforc/' | relative_url }})). Rather than acting as a simple count of unexplained objects in the sky, NUFORC records when people decide to interpret an experience as noteworthy and submit a report. That decision is strongly influenced by public attention, media narratives, internet search activity and wider social conditions. Researchers therefore treat attention itself as an observable variable that helps explain reporting surges, rather than assuming every spike reflects a genuine increase in [unidentified]({{ 'unidentified/' | relative_url }}) objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-49527-x" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">An environmental analysis of public UAP sightings and sky view potential &#124; Scientific ReportsDecember 14, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 14, 2023</span></span></span>

## Can news coverage create a reporting wave?

The evidence suggests that it can. Publicity changes what people notice, how they interpret ambiguous sights, and whether they think a reporting organisation is worth contacting. A major news story, documentary, government announcement or viral social media discussion can encourage both immediate reports and submissions describing events that occurred weeks, months or even years earlier.

This creates a well-known feedback mechanism:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. A widely reported UFO-related event attracts public attention.
2. More people begin watching the sky or reconsider earlier experiences.
3. Ambiguous lights, satellites, aircraft or astronomical objects are more likely to be interpreted as unusual.
4. Witnesses become more aware of reporting systems such as NUFORC.
5. Submission numbers rise, even if the underlying rate of aerial phenomena has not changed.

</div>

Because NUFORC records voluntary submissions rather than systematically sampled observations, these behavioural changes directly affect the database.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-49527-x" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">An environmental analysis of public UAP sightings and sky view potential &#124; Scientific ReportsDecember 14, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 14, 2023</span></span></span>

### Historical reporting waves

The pattern is visible throughout the history of modern UFO reporting. Kenneth Arnold's widely publicised 1947 sighting rapidly introduced the phrase "flying saucer" into popular culture and was followed by a dramatic increase in similar reports across the United States. Historians and social scientists have long debated whether this represented a sudden increase in unusual observations or a shift in public interpretation driven by extensive media attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wired.com/story/how-ufo-sightings-became-an-american-obsession" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">How UFO Sightings Became an American Obsession</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The period following his sighting saw a surge in UFO sightings across the U.S. This surge, or &quot;flap,&quot; is linked to societal fears of inva...</span></span></span>

Later periods show similar dynamics following highly publicised television programmes, books, government disclosures and, more recently, coverage of U.S. military videos and congressional hearings. These episodes repeatedly coincide with renewed public interest and increased reporting activity, although the existence of more reports alone does not establish that more unexplained phenomena occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-04799-8" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Exploring expert figures in alien-related UFO conspiracy theories &#124; Humanities and Social Sciences CommunicationsApril 15, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 15, 2025</span></span></span>

## Media exposure and delayed submissions

One feature of NUFORC that complicates time-series analysis is that many reports are submitted well after the event itself. Publicity often prompts people to report experiences they had previously dismissed or never mentioned.

For researchers, this distinction is critical:

* **Event date** estimates when the witness says the observation occurred.
* **Submission date** measures when the witness became motivated to report it.

A major news story can therefore produce an apparent reporting surge even though many of the underlying observations happened months or years earlier. If analysts examine posting dates alone, publicity-driven backlogs can resemble sudden increases in sightings. This is why studies using NUFORC data typically distinguish between occurrence dates and reporting dates wherever possible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-49527-x" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">An environmental analysis of public UAP sightings and sky view potential &#124; Scientific ReportsDecember 14, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 14, 2023</span></span></span>

## Online attention and changing social conditions

Modern reporting behaviour is influenced not only by traditional news but also by online search activity, social media and wider cultural interest.

Researchers increasingly use measures such as Google Trends because internet searches provide an independent indicator of public attention. Rising search volumes for terms such as "UFO" or "UAP" frequently accompany major media events, allowing investigators to compare changing interest with changes in reporting behaviour rather than assuming reports alone measure unusual aerial activity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-04799-8" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Exploring expert figures in alien-related UFO conspiracy theories &#124; Humanities and Social Sciences CommunicationsApril 15, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 15, 2025</span></span></span>

Social conditions also affect reporting independently of any change in the sky itself. Factors include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* greater public awareness of reporting organisations;
* increased internet access and easier online submission;
* widespread discussion on social media;
* documentaries, streaming series and podcasts;
* government announcements that reduce perceived stigma around reporting;
* periods when people spend more time outdoors or looking at the night sky.

</div>

The 2023 national-scale analysis of NUFORC data explicitly notes that reporting databases represent volunteered geographic information. Like other crowdsourced datasets, they reflect both environmental opportunity and human reporting behaviour, making social influences part of the data-generating process rather than external noise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-49527-x" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">An environmental analysis of public UAP sightings and sky view potential &#124; Scientific ReportsDecember 14, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 14, 2023</span></span></span>

## Separating publicity effects from genuine event patterns

Researchers do not simply discard reporting spikes because publicity may be involved. Instead, they attempt to distinguish attention effects from possible changes in observation patterns.

Common approaches include comparing:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* reported event dates with submission dates;
* regional differences rather than national totals;
* multiple independent databases instead of a single catalogue;
* known media events against reporting timelines;
* environmental variables such as cloud cover, light pollution and viewing opportunity.

</div>

For example, the large-scale environmental study of NUFORC reports found that reporting frequency is associated with factors that increase opportunities to observe the sky, including lower cloud cover and reduced tree canopy, alongside human variables such as population and aircraft activity. The authors also note that internet availability likely influenced reporting, particularly during the early years of widespread [online reporting]({{ 'online-reporting/' | relative_url }}). These findings reinforce that reported sightings arise from both external events and human opportunity to observe and report them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-49527-x" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">An environmental analysis of public UAP sightings and sky view potential &#124; Scientific ReportsDecember 14, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 14, 2023</span></span></span>

Researchers are therefore cautious about interpreting isolated national peaks. A sudden increase following a major documentary or government hearing may represent heightened awareness rather than an equivalent increase in unexplained aerial phenomena.

## What attention surges mean for interpreting NUFORC

Media-driven reporting waves do not make NUFORC unreliable; they simply define what the database measures. NUFORC is best understood as a record of reported experiences occurring within changing social and cultural environments.

For investigators, attention surges are valuable in their own right because they reveal how public awareness shapes voluntary reporting. They also remind analysts that the database measures two intertwined processes: what witnesses believe they observed and when they chose to tell someone about it. Separating those processes is essential before drawing conclusions about long-term trends or apparent waves of UFO activity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-49527-x" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">An environmental analysis of public UAP sightings and sky view potential &#124; Scientific ReportsDecember 14, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 14, 2023</span></span></span>
