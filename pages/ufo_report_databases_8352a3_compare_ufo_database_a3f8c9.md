## Introduction

UFO databases cannot be ranked fairly by headline totals or by the percentage of cases labelled “unexplained”. A public reporting site, an investigative agency and a historical archive are counting different things. One may record every submitted narrative, another may publish only cases that have passed through a formal inquiry, while a third preserves documents created under a discontinued government programme. Their totals therefore describe the reach and rules of the repository, not the underlying frequency of unusual objects.

The right database depends on the question. The National UFO Reporting Center, or NUFORC, is well suited to studying public reporting patterns and witness language. France’s [GEIPAN]({{ 'geipan/' | relative_url }}) is stronger for examining documented investigations and classification decisions. [Project Blue Book]({{ 'blue-book/' | relative_url }}) and national archives are most useful for reconstructing how governments handled reports in particular periods. MUFON and UFOCAT offer extensive civilian research material, but access restrictions, uneven documentation and deliberate duplication make their headline counts especially easy to misread.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/databank/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Data Bank &#124; NUFORC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Data Bank &#124; NUFORC - Latest UFO SightingsThe NUFORC Databank is the largest independently collected set of UFO / UAP sighting repor...</span></span></span>

## Public Reports, Investigations and Archives

The first comparison to make is not between database sizes, but between their **units of collection**. Is one row a witness submission, an investigated incident, a source reference or an archival file? Those categories may look similar in a search result, yet they support very different conclusions.

**[NUFORC]({{ 'nuforc/' | relative_url }}) records public submissions.** Its reporting form asks witnesses for the event date, time, location, duration, number of witnesses, description and supporting media. Its public indexes organise reports by date, location and other fields, making the collection useful for tracing when people reported certain shapes, descriptions or clusters of events. The underlying population, however, consists of people who knew about NUFORC and chose to contact it; it is not a complete sample of everyone who saw something unusual.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/reportform/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[NUFORC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Sighting Report Form &#124; NUFORCNational UFO Reporting Center – Sighting Report Form · SIGHTING DATE AND TIME · SIGHTING LOCATION...</span></span></span>

That distinction matters even within a single entry. NUFORC displays both when an event allegedly occurred and when it was reported. A recollection submitted decades later may sit beside an account filed the same evening. Researchers interested in media-driven reporting waves, memory effects or seasonal submission patterns must therefore separate event date from submission date rather than treating all records as contemporaneous observations. NUFORC itself periodically adds large batches of reports, so a spike in database publication can also reflect processing schedules rather than a sudden wave of sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/1039-new-reports-posted/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[NUFORC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1039 new reports posted</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1039 new reports posted</span></span></span>

**[MUFON]({{ 'mufon/' | relative_url }}) combines submissions with volunteer investigation.** Its Case Management System stores the original witness form and, where a case is followed up, a field investigator’s findings. MUFON describes separate forms for issues such as landing traces, electromagnetic effects, animal reactions and other reported features. This creates richer potential case files than a narrative-only repository, but the depth of investigation can vary according to investigator availability, witness cooperation and the evidence supplied.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/JULY_2023_MUFON_Journal_WEB.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mufon.com">[MUFON]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">A NEWCOMER&#x27;S GUIDE TO MUFON</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A NEWCOMER&#x27;S GUIDE TO MUFON</span></span></span>

MUFON’s database is also not equivalent to a fully open public dataset. The organisation states that active paid members may access specified parts of the system, including narratives and attachments, while reuse and distribution are restricted by its terms. Consequently, an outside researcher may be able to inspect selected recent reports without having the same access needed to audit classifications, attachments or historical coverage across the whole collection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mufon.com/search_database-terms-and-conditions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mufon.com">[MUFON]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Search Database Terms and ConditionsThe Mutual UFO Network (MUFON) allows active members to access certain parts of our Case Managem...</span></span></span>

**GEIPAN publishes investigated cases under a defined classification system.** The French agency assesses both the consistency of the information and the residual strangeness after inquiry. Its familiar A, B, C and D classes distinguish identified cases, probably identified cases, cases lacking sufficient reliable information and cases remaining [unidentified]({{ 'unidentified/' | relative_url }}) after investigation. The resulting database is therefore not simply an inbox of public claims: its labels reflect a stated evaluation process.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/index.php/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Classification8. Even here, “unidentified” does not mean “extraordinary craft”. It means that the agency did not establish an adequate conventional explanation from the available evidence under its own method. A case can remain unresolved because observations are unusual and well documented, but another repository might leave a similar report unresolved because no one investigated it at all. Counting both as the same category would erase the most important methodological difference.

**Government archives preserve records rather than operate live reporting systems.** The US National Archives holds Project Blue Book material covering reports investigated by the US Air Force from 1947 to 1969. The UK National Archives holds Ministry of Defence sighting reports, correspondence, policy papers and parliamentary material. These collections answer historical questions about what officials received, recorded, investigated and retained; they do not provide a current, uniform multinational census of sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects25 Jun 2024 — From 1947 to 1969, a total of 12, 618 sightings were report...</span></span></span>

Archives also organise information at several levels. One catalogue record may describe a folder, microfilm roll or series containing numerous reports. Conversely, one incident may produce several documents: a witness letter, police communication, internal memorandum, technical assessment and later correspondence. Counting catalogue entries as sightings would therefore be as misleading as counting every page in a case file as a separate event.

**[UFOCAT]({{ 'ufocat/' | relative_url }}) is primarily a reference catalogue.** The Center for UFO Studies explicitly warns that it often contains multiple records for the same sighting because its purpose was to index all available sources: an original file, a periodical article and a later book account might each receive an entry. Duplication is therefore partly a design feature rather than simply poor data cleaning. UFOCAT is valuable for locating the literature around a case, but its total number of records should not be treated as a total number of unique incidents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/cufos-publications-databases/ufocat/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesUFOCATUFOCAT is a catalog of published and unpublished UFO sighting reports. ## Why Totals Are Not Directly Comparable

A database total is produced by a chain of filters. Comparing two totals without mapping those filters is like comparing hospital admissions with calls to a medical advice line: both concern health events, but they do not count the same population.

### Intake changes the population

NUFORC and MUFON depend heavily on voluntary witness reporting. Their geographical distributions are shaped by population, internet use, language, publicity, familiarity with the organisation and confidence that a report will be taken seriously. A high count in one US state may reflect more observers and greater reporting awareness rather than a higher incidence of anomalous objects. Academic work using NUFORC records therefore controls for environmental, demographic and visibility-related variables rather than interpreting raw counts as direct exposure rates.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10721628/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed Central (PMC)]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nih.gov.</span></span></span>

Government programmes use different gates. Project Blue Book received reports within a military and national-security framework, during a specific period and under reporting procedures that changed over time. The British Ministry of Defence similarly collected public correspondence and reports for administrative and defence purposes. Neither collection should be compared directly with a modern global web form that can accept a retrospective account from almost anywhere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects25 Jun 2024 — From 1947 to 1969, a total of 12, 618 sightings were report...</span></span></span>

Geography is another built-in limit. GEIPAN is a French national service, whereas NUFORC’s interface is worldwide but its data are heavily centred on the United States. MUFON describes an international network, yet its membership, chapters and reporting visibility are not evenly distributed worldwide. A database’s map is therefore partly a map of its institutional footprint.

### One incident can become several records

Duplicate counting is one of the largest obstacles to combining repositories. Several witnesses may report the same event independently. The same witness may submit to both NUFORC and MUFON. A newspaper report may enter UFOCAT separately from an investigator’s case summary. A government archive may preserve several documents about the same occurrence.

Simple deduplication by date and place is unsafe. Two unrelated observations may occur in the same city on the same evening, while reports of one event may contain different times, spellings or locations. Retrospective reports further complicate matching because dates may be approximate. Reliable linkage generally requires comparison of narrative details, direction of travel, duration, witness count, geography, source lineage and submission history. Even then, the result may be a probability that two records match, not a certainty.

This is why a merged “mega-database” needs an explicit distinction between **records**, **reports**, **witnesses**, **events** and **source references**. Without it, a collection may become larger while its statistical meaning becomes weaker.

### “Unexplained” is not a universal outcome

Project Blue Book recorded 12,618 reports, of which 701 were left “unidentified”, roughly 5.6 per cent. That figure is often compared with rates from other catalogues, but it belongs to the Air Force’s own procedures, priorities, available information and historical period. It cannot be treated as a universal baseline for how many UFO reports resist explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects25 Jun 2024 — From 1947 to 1969, a total of 12, 618 sightings were report...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN’s D classification is also method-specific. The agency considers the consistency of the case and the strangeness remaining after investigation. NUFORC, by contrast, publishes many first-hand reports without assigning every entry a completed investigative disposition. A blank explanation field in NUFORC is therefore not equivalent to a GEIPAN D case or a Blue Book “unidentified”. One may indicate “not formally resolved in this public record”; another means “investigated but not adequately identified under this system”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/index.php/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Classification8. The denominator can be just as misleading as the category. Some systems include information-poor reports; others classify them separately or exclude them from stronger unresolved categories. A repository that retains every vague account may show a lower proportion of compelling [unexplained cases]({{ 'unexplained-cases/' | relative_url }}) because its denominator is large. A curated catalogue focused on unusual cases may show a much higher rate because ordinary or readily explained reports were never admitted.

### Investigation effort affects the result

An unexplained rate partly measures how much identifying work was attempted. Checking astronomy, aircraft traffic, satellite passes, weather, balloons, drones and local events can convert an initially puzzling report into an identified one. GEIPAN’s case pages may record a proposed phenomenon and subsequent revision, whereas an uninvestigated witness narrative may preserve the original uncertainty indefinitely.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/search/cas?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=116&amp;order=field_classification_des_cas&amp;page=%2C12&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This produces an apparent paradox: the more thoroughly a database investigates ordinary causes, the fewer unexplained cases it may retain. A lower unresolved percentage can therefore signal stronger identification work rather than less interesting raw material. Conversely, a high unresolved percentage may reflect exceptional cases, weak evidence, insufficient investigative resources or a classification rule that leaves ambiguity open. The percentage alone cannot distinguish among those explanations.

### Access determines what can be checked

Open browsing is not the same as open data. NUFORC makes individual reports and indexes publicly visible, but researchers still need to document how records were collected, cleaned and transformed. MUFON restricts broader database access and reuse. Project Blue Book’s complete textual record historically required navigating 94 microfilm rolls, although archive digitisation and bulk-access initiatives are improving access to some US UAP-related holdings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/databank/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Data Bank &#124; NUFORC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Data Bank &#124; NUFORC - Latest UFO SightingsThe NUFORC Databank is the largest independently collected set of UFO / UAP sighting repor...</span></span></span>

## Matching a Database to Research Goals

No single repository is “best” in the abstract. A useful choice begins with the claim a researcher hopes to make.

**For studying what witnesses report:** NUFORC is usually the strongest starting point because its public records preserve large numbers of first-person descriptions, event and submission dates, reported shapes and locations. It can support research into language, reporting waves, temporal patterns and public attention. Conclusions should be framed as patterns in submitted reports, not estimates of the true incidence of unexplained objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/ndx/?id=event" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[NUFORC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC Reports by MonthNUFORC Reports by Month SIGHTING YEAR/MONTH REPORT COUNT 2026/06 224 2026/05 296 2026/04 226 2026/03 208 202...</span></span></span>

**For comparing investigative outcomes:** GEIPAN is better suited because it states its classification principles and publishes case material linked to investigative decisions. Researchers can examine why one report was identified, another judged probably identified, another left without adequate information and another retained as unidentified. Its national scope limits broad international inference, but that narrower population makes its internal procedures more interpretable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/index.php/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Classification8. **For civilian casework with attachments:** MUFON may offer detailed witness forms, investigator reports and media. It is potentially useful for close case analysis, particularly where field investigation was completed. The trade-off is that comprehensive access and permitted reuse are more constrained, and the consistency of volunteer investigation must be assessed rather than assumed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mufon.com/search_database-terms-and-conditions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mufon.com">[MUFON]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Search Database Terms and ConditionsThe Mutual UFO Network (MUFON) allows active members to access certain parts of our Case Managem...</span></span></span>

**For historical government behaviour:** Project Blue Book, British Ministry of Defence files and similar national archives are the appropriate sources. They can show reporting channels, institutional priorities, internal discussion, contemporary explanations and policy changes. Their surviving documents should be treated as administrative records produced by particular agencies, not as neutral samples of all sightings that occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects25 Jun 2024 — From 1947 to 1969, a total of 12, 618 sightings were report...</span></span></span>

**For tracing a case through books, journals and private files:** UFOCAT’s source-oriented structure is an advantage. Multiple entries may reveal how an incident was documented and retold across decades. Researchers must consolidate those references at the incident level before calculating frequencies or geographical patterns.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/cufos-publications-databases/ufocat/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesUFOCATUFOCAT is a catalog of published and unpublished UFO sighting reports. **For cross-database statistical work:** the original classifications should be preserved rather than forced immediately into a single “explained/unexplained” column. A defensible harmonised dataset should retain, at minimum:

* the source database and its version or access date;

* whether the row represents a submission, witness, incident, investigation or document;
* event date, submission date and publication date as separate fields;
* geographical precision and national coverage;
* investigation status and investigator type;
* the repository’s original classification and its definition;
* evidence types available, missing or inaccessible;

The safest comparison is often within one repository across time or geography, using stable rules. Cross-repository comparisons become more credible only after the populations have been narrowed to genuinely comparable cases—for example, recent first-hand reports with precise dates and locations, or formally investigated cases with sufficient documentation. Even then, the result describes the selected records under the chosen harmonisation rules, not an objective worldwide rate of UFO activity.

## The Comparison That Matters Most

The most meaningful question is not “Which database has the most UFOs?” but “What process produced each record?” NUFORC measures voluntary public reporting. MUFON adds a variable layer of civilian investigation. GEIPAN publishes cases assessed under a government methodology. Project Blue Book and national archives preserve historically bounded official records. UFOCAT maps references and [source trails]({{ 'source-trails/' | relative_url }}), sometimes intentionally multiplying entries for one incident.

Once those functions are separated, apparent contradictions become easier to understand. A very large public database can be broad but weak for determining final explanations. A smaller investigated collection can support stronger case comparisons but cover only one jurisdiction. An archive can be authoritative about what an agency recorded without being comprehensive about what occurred. An unresolved label can represent residual strangeness after inquiry, insufficient information, unfinished work or simply the absence of a published explanation.

Database totals and unexplained percentages become useful only after intake, geography, duplication, investigation, classification and access have been matched. Until then, they are properties of different systems—not directly comparable measurements of the same phenomenon.
