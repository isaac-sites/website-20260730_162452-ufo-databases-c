## Introduction

[UFOCAT]({{ 'ufocat/' | relative_url }}) survived not because it was preserved by a single institution with a formal digital archive, but because civilian researchers repeatedly rescued it from obsolete computer systems and ageing storage media. During the 1980s and early 1990s, the catalogue faced a genuine risk of partial or permanent loss after it became too expensive for the Center for UFO Studies (CUFOS) to maintain on an IBM mainframe. What exists today is the result of a series of recoveries involving magnetic tapes, incomplete floppy-disk copies and painstaking reconstruction rather than a seamless technological upgrade. That rescue effort illustrates a broader challenge faced by many early research databases: preserving the data often depended more on committed individuals than on institutional infrastructure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/cufos-publications-databases/ufocat/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies UFOCAT</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesUFOCAT - Center for UFO Studies...</span></span></span>

## The Mainframe Catalogue and Its Storage Crisis

When David Saunders transferred UFOCAT to CUFOS in 1976, the catalogue was maintained on an IBM mainframe computer at a nearby computing facility. During this period, updates were managed by Fred Merritt, while routine protection relied on magnetic-tape backups—the standard archival technology for large computer systems of the era. Magnetic tape offered reliable long-term storage but required specialised hardware, software and operators to read and maintain it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/cufos-publications-databases/ufocat/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies UFOCAT</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesUFOCAT - Center for UFO Studies...</span></span></span>

For a small civilian research organisation, those arrangements gradually became unsustainable. By 1982, the costs of operating and updating the database on a commercial mainframe exceeded CUFOS's resources. Rather than continue active maintenance, the catalogue was removed from everyday use and archived onto magnetic tapes. This decision preserved the information in one sense, but also trapped it inside a technological environment that was rapidly disappearing as personal computers replaced institutional mainframes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/cufos-publications-databases/ufocat/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies UFOCAT</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesUFOCAT - Center for UFO Studies...</span></span></span>

The storage problem was therefore not simply physical preservation. The tapes themselves survived, but accessing their contents increasingly required obsolete equipment and compatible software. Like many digital archives created during the 1970s and early 1980s, UFOCAT faced the risk that functioning readers would disappear before anyone could migrate the data into newer formats.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ibm.com/history/magnetic-tape" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ibm.com">[IBM]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ibm.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Magnetic tape &#124; IBM</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Magnetic tape &#124; IBM...</span></span></span>

## Reconstructing the Damaged Backup

The decisive rescue began in 1990 when Donald Johnson obtained a copy of UFOCAT distributed across ten 3.5-inch floppy diskettes. Those diskettes had been created by John Derr of the U.S. Geological Survey, who had converted one of the original magnetic-tape backups for his own research. However, the copy proved incomplete because the first portion of the tape could not be read during the conversion, leaving approximately the first 10,000 records missing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/UFOCAT%20Codebook%202023.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USER&#x27;S GUIDE UFOCAT 2023 Concord, New Ham...</span></span></span>

Instead of accepting the damaged copy, Johnson searched for additional surviving material. Mark Rodeghier, then Director of CUFOS, located an older magnetic-tape backup that still contained the missing records. Johnson merged the two independent versions, using each to compensate for the weaknesses of the other. The result was a reconstructed catalogue that closely matched the database as it had existed when active maintenance ceased in 1982.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/UFOCAT%20Codebook%202023.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USER&#x27;S GUIDE UFOCAT 2023 Concord, New Ham...</span></span></span>

This recovery demonstrates an important feature of early digital preservation. The successful reconstruction did not depend on a perfect master copy surviving intact. Instead, it relied on preserving multiple imperfect copies created at different times and for different purposes. Only by comparing and combining those versions could researchers restore a substantially complete catalogue.

The episode also highlights how informal research networks became part of the preservation process. The recovery involved university researchers, CUFOS staff and a scientist at the U.S. Geological Survey, none of whom were operating within a dedicated digital preservation programme. Their overlapping personal copies ultimately became the archive's safety net.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/UFOCAT%20Codebook%202023.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USER&#x27;S GUIDE UFOCAT 2023 Concord, New Ham...</span></span></span>

## Moving the Data into Modern Formats

Once the missing records had been restored, the greater challenge was preventing the same crisis from recurring. Rather than continue relying on mainframe-era file structures, Johnson migrated UFOCAT into contemporary desktop database software, first using dBase IV and later Microsoft Access. These systems could run on ordinary personal computers, dramatically lowering maintenance costs while making routine updates practical once again.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/cufos-publications-databases/ufocat/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies UFOCAT</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesUFOCAT - Center for UFO Studies...</span></span></span>

The migration involved more than copying records into a new program. Earlier versions of UFOCAT used many abbreviated, single-character codes inherited from the constraints of older computing environments. During conversion, these fields were expanded into more descriptive names, new data fields were introduced and considerably greater emphasis was placed on documenting the bibliographic source for every catalogue entry. These changes made the database easier to understand while preserving compatibility with earlier records wherever possible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/cufos-publications-databases/ufocat/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies UFOCAT</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesUFOCAT - Center for UFO Studies...</span></span></span>

The catalogue continued evolving after the initial migration. Later releases were distributed in Microsoft Access format alongside spreadsheet versions, allowing researchers without specialist database software to examine the underlying records. By moving away from proprietary mainframe environments and towards widely supported desktop formats, the custodians substantially reduced the risk that UFOCAT would again become inaccessible solely because of obsolete hardware or software.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/cufos-publications-databases/ufocat/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies UFOCAT</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesUFOCAT - Center for UFO Studies...</span></span></span>

## Why the Rescue Matters


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The survival of UFOCAT illustrates a broader lesson in digital preservation. Digital information does not remain accessible simply because storage media endure. Hardware becomes obsolete, software disappears, file formats change and undocumented encoding systems become difficult to interpret. Preservation therefore requires periodic migration rather than passive storage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2015/05/saving-the-digital-record/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.harvard.edu">[Harvard Gazette]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.harvard.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gazette Saving the digital record — Harvard Gazette</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Harvard GazetteSaving the digital record — Harvard Gazette...</span></span></span>
