## Introduction

[UFOCAT]({{ 'ufocat/' | relative_url }}) is more than a catalogue of reported UFO events: it is also a catalogue of sources, and those sources are not equally accessible. One of the database's most important but often overlooked features is that it distinguishes between published material, privately held investigator files and records subject to confidentiality restrictions. This means that a UFOCAT entry tells researchers not only where information originated, but also whether they are likely to inspect the underlying evidence themselves or whether access depends on the permission of an individual investigator or organisation. The result is that two records describing similar incidents may differ greatly in how independently verifiable they are. The UFOCAT codebook explicitly treats these access distinctions as part of the database's design rather than an afterthought.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/UFOCAT%20Codebook%202023.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USER&#x27;S GUIDE UFOCAT 2023 Concord, New HamNovember 7, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2025</span></span></span>

## Which UFOCAT Sources Can Researchers Actually Check?

The practical value of a UFOCAT source reference depends on what type of material it identifies. The catalogue separates sources according to their origin because each category carries different expectations about availability, completeness and independent verification.

### Published, private and organisational source types

Published sources are generally the easiest to verify. These include newspapers, magazines, books, journals and organisational newsletters that can often be located in libraries, archives or digital collections. When a UFOCAT record cites published material, another researcher can usually inspect the same document and compare interpretations directly.

Private investigator files occupy a different category. These may include:

* witness interview notes;
* correspondence;
* sketches and photographs;
* field investigation reports;
* unpublished case summaries.

Such material may contain substantially than a published article, but it often remains in the custody of the investigator or the organisation that collected it. Access therefore depends on whether the owner is willing to share the records. UFOCAT preserves references to these files even when they are not publicly available because they document that additional evidence exists, although not necessarily that every researcher can inspect it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/UFOCAT%20Codebook%202023.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USER&#x27;S GUIDE UFOCAT 2023 Concord, New HamNovember 7, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2025</span></span></span>

Organisational archives occupy an intermediate position. Collections maintained by groups such as the Center for UFO Studies (CUFOS) may preserve decades of historical investigation files, yet access can still be governed by archive policies, donor agreements or privacy commitments rather than open public inspection. Consequently, a catalogue reference may identify a real archival source without implying unrestricted availability.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/UFOCAT%20Codebook%202023.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USER&#x27;S GUIDE UFOCAT 2023 Concord, New HamNovember 7, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2025</span></span></span>

### Restricted witness details and confidential cases

One distinctive feature of the UFOCAT codebook is its formal treatment of confidentiality. Rather than simply omitting sensitive material, the database assigns availability levels that specify how much information may be released.

The codebook explains that contributors can choose different "levels of availability" for unpublished material. Published records remain freely available, while unpublished records may be restricted in several ways, beginning with the removal of witness names and extending to broader limitations on access. This system was designed to respect promises made to witnesses and investigators who supplied their files on the understanding that sensitive information would not automatically become public.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/UFOCAT%20Codebook%202023.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USER&#x27;S GUIDE UFOCAT 2023 Concord, New HamNovember 7, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2025</span></span></span>

In practice, this means a researcher may encounter entries where:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* witness names have been replaced by initials;
* identifying personal information has been removed;
* the source file exists but cannot be freely copied;
* permission from the contributing investigator is required before additional documentation can be examined.

</div>

The codebook also notes that contributors concerned about inappropriate use of their material could effectively classify their submissions by choosing more restrictive availability levels. At the same time, CUFOS reserved the right to reject submissions carrying excessive restrictions, recognising that a research database loses value if its contents become largely inaccessible. According to the codebook, fewer than ten per cent of primary records imposed restrictions beyond withholding witness names, with the intention of reducing that proportion over time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/UFOCAT%20Codebook%202023.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USER&#x27;S GUIDE UFOCAT 2023 Concord, New HamNovember 7, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2025</span></span></span>

## How Access Limits Affect Verification

These access categories have important consequences for anyone evaluating UFO evidence.

First, a catalogue citation does not guarantee that the underlying documentation can be independently inspected. A published newspaper article may be available immediately, whereas an investigator's original interview notes may remain inaccessible decades after the case entered UFOCAT.

Second, access restrictions affect reproducibility. Historical research relies on other investigators being able to examine the same documents and judge whether conclusions are justified. When only summaries or secondary accounts are publicly available, independent verification becomes more difficult because the original interview records, correspondence or photographs cannot necessarily be reviewed.

Third, differences in accessibility should not automatically be interpreted as differences in credibility. Confidentiality often reflects ethical obligations rather than evidential weakness. Witnesses may have requested anonymity, investigators may have promised privacy, or organisations may be preserving personal records under archival agreements. The existence of restricted material therefore says more about record governance than about whether a reported event was accurately described.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/UFOCAT%20Codebook%202023.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USER&#x27;S GUIDE UFOCAT 2023 Concord, New HamNovember 7, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2025</span></span></span>

For this reason, experienced researchers distinguish between three separate questions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Does the catalogue identify a source?** UFOCAT usually answers this clearly.
* **Can the original source be examined today?** The answer depends on its availability category.
